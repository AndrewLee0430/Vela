## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and increased risk of hypercalcemia. Concomitant use of calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers may reduce the antihypertensive effect of the latter, and with thiazide diuretics (such as hydrochlorothiazide), there is an increased risk of hypercalcemia, necessitating close monitoring of calcium levels [1], [2].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers if possible, as calcium may reduce the response to these antihypertensive agents. If concomitant use is unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration [1].
- **Thiazide Diuretics (e.g., Hydrochlorothiazide):** Thiazide diuretics can increase calcium levels. When used with calcium chloride, there is a higher risk of hypercalcemia. Increase the frequency of monitoring serum calcium concentrations in these patients [1], [2].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** Additional medications such as vitamin D, vitamin A, lithium, and certain hormones also increase the risk of hypercalcemia when combined with calcium chloride. Monitor calcium levels more frequently if these are used together [1].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride and digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration [1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - Check serum calcium at baseline and regularly during therapy, especially if used with thiazide diuretics or other drugs that increase calcium.
  - Monitor blood pressure closely if calcium is used with calcium channel blockers.
  - Monitor ECG if calcium is used with digoxin.
- **Elderly Considerations:** Elderly patients are at increased risk for drug interactions and adverse effects, including electrolyte disturbances. Frequent monitoring and dose adjustments may be necessary.
