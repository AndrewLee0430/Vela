## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections together with blood pressure medications that are calcium channel blockers, as concomitant use may reduce the effectiveness of the blood pressure medication and requires close blood pressure monitoring if unavoidable[1]. The interaction is due to calcium potentially antagonizing the effects of calcium channel blockers.

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Safety Warnings:** Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers in elderly patients, as this may reduce the antihypertensive effect of the blood pressure medication[1].
- **Key Contraindications:** If the combination cannot be avoided, monitor blood pressure closely during administration of calcium chloride injection[1].
- **Drug Interactions:** Calcium chloride injection can also interact with digoxin, increasing the risk of arrhythmias and digoxin toxicity, especially in the presence of hypercalcemia. ECG monitoring is recommended if used together[1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** For patients on calcium chloride injection and other drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium), increase the frequency of calcium concentration monitoring[1].
- **Missing Information:** The provided context does not specify interactions with other classes of blood pressure medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, ARBs, diuretics other than thiazides), so recommendations are limited to calcium channel blockers.
