## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and increased risk of hypercalcemia. Calcium chloride can interact with several BP medications, especially thiazide diuretics and calcium channel blockers, necessitating close monitoring of calcium levels and cardiovascular status [2], [3].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Concomitant use of calcium chloride and thiazide diuretics (such as hydrochlorothiazide) increases the risk of hypercalcemia; monitor serum calcium concentrations more frequently in these patients [2].
- Calcium chloride should generally be avoided with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, continuous ECG monitoring is recommended during administration [2].
- Calcium chloride can reduce the antihypertensive effect of calcium channel blockers; if co-administration is necessary, monitor blood pressure closely during treatment [2], [3].
- Other drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., Vitamin D, parathyroid hormone, lithium) require increased frequency of calcium monitoring when used with calcium chloride [2].
- In elderly patients, who may have reduced renal function and altered drug metabolism, the risk of adverse effects from these interactions is heightened. Regular monitoring of renal function, serum electrolytes (especially calcium), and cardiovascular status is advised.
- There is insufficient context regarding specific BP medications beyond thiazide diuretics and calcium channel blockers, so caution should be exercised with other antihypertensives until further evidence is available.
