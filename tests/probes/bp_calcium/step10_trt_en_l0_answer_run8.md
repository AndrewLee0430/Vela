## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections concomitantly with calcium channel blockers, as calcium chloride may reduce the response to these antihypertensive medications. If concomitant use is unavoidable, close blood pressure monitoring is required during administration. There is no direct evidence in the provided context regarding oral calcium supplements with other classes of blood pressure medications in the elderly, so caution and individualized assessment are advised[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers (such as amlodipine, nifedipine, verapamil, diltiazem) may reduce the antihypertensive effect of these drugs. Avoid this combination if possible; if unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration[1].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection with digoxin, as this combination increases the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and digoxin toxicity due to potential hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration[1].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** If calcium chloride is used with drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., calcipotriene, estrogen, lithium, parathyroid hormone, teriparatide, thiazide diuretics, Vitamin A, Vitamin D), increase the frequency of serum calcium monitoring[1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - For patients on calcium chloride injection and interacting medications, monitor blood pressure (if on calcium channel blockers), ECG (if on digoxin), and serum calcium levels (if on other hypercalcemia-risk drugs) during and after administration[1].
- **Missing Information:** The provided context does not address the safety or interactions of oral calcium supplements with antihypertensive medications other than calcium channel blockers or in the broader elderly population. There is also no information on other classes of blood pressure medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics except thiazides) in relation to calcium supplementation[1][2]. 

Careful medication review and monitoring are essential when combining calcium preparations with antihypertensive therapy in elderly patients.
