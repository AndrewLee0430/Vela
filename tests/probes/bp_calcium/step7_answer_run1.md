Yes, elderly patients can generally take blood pressure (BP) medications together with calcium supplements, but there are some important considerations:

### 1. Interaction Between Calcium and BP Medications
- **Calcium and Calcium Channel Blockers:** Calcium channel blockers (e.g., amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil) are a common class of BP meds. Taking calcium supplements does not usually reduce their effectiveness, but excessive calcium intake might theoretically affect vascular tone. This is usually not clinically significant if calcium intake is within recommended limits.
- **Calcium and Other BP Medications:** Calcium supplements generally do not have significant interactions with other classes such as ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, or diuretics.

### 2. Absorption Issues
- Calcium supplements can interfere with the absorption of some medications if taken simultaneously. For example:
  - **Calcium can reduce absorption of certain drugs** if taken at the same time (e.g., some antibiotics like tetracyclines or fluoroquinolones).
  - For BP meds, this is usually not a major concern, but spacing doses apart by 1-2 hours is a reasonable precaution.

### 3. Risk of Hypercalcemia
- Elderly patients, especially those with kidney impairment, are at risk of hypercalcemia if calcium supplements are overused.
- Hypercalcemia can affect heart rhythm and blood pressure control, so monitoring calcium levels may be necessary.

### 4. Bone Health Considerations
- Calcium supplements are often prescribed to elderly patients for osteoporosis prevention or treatment.
- Ensuring adequate calcium intake is important, but total daily calcium (diet + supplements) should not exceed recommended levels (usually around 1000-1200 mg/day for elderly).

### 5. Clinical Recommendations
- Elderly patients on BP meds can take calcium supplements safely if:
  - They follow prescribed doses.
  - They space calcium supplements and BP meds appropriately if advised.
  - Their kidney function and serum calcium are monitored periodically.
  - They inform their healthcare provider about all medications and supplements.

### Summary
- There is no absolute contraindication to taking BP meds with calcium supplements in elderly patients.
- Careful management and monitoring are advised to avoid potential absorption issues or hypercalcemia.
- Always consult the prescribing physician or pharmacist for personalized advice.
