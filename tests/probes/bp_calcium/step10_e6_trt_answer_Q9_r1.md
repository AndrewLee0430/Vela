## Summary

Daily low-dose aspirin (100 mg) does not significantly affect blood pressure (BP) in community-dwelling older adults, including those on antihypertensive therapy, with mean differences in systolic and diastolic BP change between aspirin and placebo groups being very small and not statistically significant (systolic: -0.03 mm Hg, 95% CI: -0.13 to 0.07; diastolic: -0.05 mm Hg, 95% CI: -0.11 to 0.01) over 4.7 years of follow-up [1]. There is also no difference in rates of incident treated hypertension between aspirin and placebo [1]. However, in patients with severe heart failure, higher-dose aspirin (325 mg) may blunt the systemic vasodilatory effect of ACE inhibitors like enalapril, likely due to prostaglandin synthesis inhibition [4].

## Clinical Notes

- **Safety and When Not to Use:** Aspirin should be used cautiously in older adults with increased bleeding risk or a history of gastrointestinal bleeding. The provided evidence does not address bleeding risk, but this remains a key concern in clinical practice.
- **Key Contraindications:** Avoid aspirin in patients with active peptic ulcer disease, known aspirin allergy, or significant bleeding disorders. In patients with severe heart failure on ACE inhibitors, higher-dose aspirin (325 mg) may reduce the antihypertensive effect of ACE inhibitors due to negative interaction on prostaglandin-mediated vasodilation [4]. This interaction has not been demonstrated with low-dose aspirin (100 mg) in community-dwelling older adults without heart failure [1].
- **Drug Interactions:** Monitor for potential drug interactions with other antiplatelet agents or anticoagulants, which may increase bleeding risk.
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - For patients on both aspirin and antihypertensive therapy, routine BP monitoring as per standard hypertension guidelines is appropriate; no additional BP monitoring is required solely due to aspirin use [1].
  - In patients with heart failure on ACE inhibitors and higher-dose aspirin, monitor for reduced BP-lowering response and consider alternative antiplatelet agents if clinically indicated [4].
- **Population Applicability:** The main evidence [1] is from community-dwelling older adults in Australia and the US without cardiovascular disease, dementia, or significant disability. The heart failure/ACE inhibitor interaction data [4] is from a small, older trial and may not generalize to all elderly populations or to low-dose aspirin.

If considering aspirin for primary prevention in the elderly, the decision should be individualized, weighing cardiovascular and bleeding risks, as BP effects are negligible [1].
