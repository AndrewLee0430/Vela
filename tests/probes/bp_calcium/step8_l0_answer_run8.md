## Summary  
Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications along with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and risks of hypercalcemia. Calcium supplements or calcium chloride may interact with certain antihypertensives like thiazide diuretics and calcium channel blockers, increasing the risk of hypercalcemia or reducing drug efficacy. Close monitoring of calcium levels and cardiovascular status is essential to avoid complications such as arrhythmias or worsening hypertension control [1][2][3].

---

## Clinical Notes  
- **Drug Interactions and Contraindications:**  
  - Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride with digoxin due to risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during calcium administration [1].  
  - Avoid combining calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers as calcium may reduce the antihypertensive response; if used together, monitor blood pressure closely [1].  
  - Thiazide diuretics, commonly used for hypertension in elderly patients, can increase calcium reabsorption and risk of hypercalcemia, especially when combined with calcium supplements or vitamin D [1][3].  
  - Beta-blockers, especially non-selective ones, should be used cautiously in patients with COPD and may interact with potassium-wasting diuretics, but this is less directly related to calcium [2].  

- **Monitoring Parameters:**  
  - Monitor serum calcium concentrations frequently when calcium supplements or calcium chloride are used with BP medications, especially thiazide diuretics or drugs that increase hypercalcemia risk (e.g., vitamin D, lithium, parathyroid hormone analogs) [1][3].  
  - Monitor renal function (creatinine) as hypercalcemia can cause acute kidney injury, particularly in elderly patients with pre-existing renal impairment [3].  
  - Monitor ECG if digoxin is used concomitantly with calcium to detect arrhythmias early [1].  
  - Monitor blood pressure closely if calcium chloride is used with calcium channel blockers to ensure adequate antihypertensive effect [1].  

- **Safety Warnings:**  
  - Hypercalcemia can cause nausea, vomiting, confusion, metabolic alkalosis, and acute kidney injury, as seen in Calcium Alkali Thiazide Syndrome (CATS) in elderly patients on calcium supplements and thiazide diuretics [3].  
  - Avoid excessive calcium and vitamin D supplementation in elderly hypertensive patients on thiazides to prevent MAS/CATS [3].  
  - Potassium levels should also be monitored in patients on diuretics and corticosteroids due to risk of hypokalemia, which can complicate management [2].  

In summary, elderly patients can take BP medications with calcium but require individualized assessment, avoidance of certain combinations (e.g., calcium chloride with digoxin or calcium channel blockers), and close monitoring of calcium, renal function, and cardiovascular status to prevent adverse effects.
