## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but certain combinations—especially thiazide diuretics with calcium and vitamin D—significantly increase the risk of hypercalcemia, acute kidney injury, and metabolic alkalosis (Calcium Alkali Thiazide Syndrome, CATS) [3]. Careful selection of antihypertensive agents and close monitoring of serum calcium and renal function are essential to minimize these risks [1][3].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Key Drug Interactions and Risks:**
  - Thiazide diuretics combined with calcium and vitamin D supplements can cause severe hypercalcemia, acute kidney injury, and metabolic alkalosis (CATS) [3]. This risk is especially relevant in elderly patients, who may have reduced renal reserve.
  - Calcium chloride should be avoided with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration [1].
  - Concomitant use of calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers may reduce the antihypertensive effect of the latter; monitor blood pressure closely if used together [1].
  - Other drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., calcipotriene, estrogen, lithium, parathyroid hormone, teriparatide, thiazide diuretics, vitamin A, and vitamin D) require increased frequency of serum calcium monitoring when used with calcium chloride [1].

- **Contraindications and Cautions:**
  - Avoid thiazide diuretics in patients already at risk for hypercalcemia or with a history of calcium alkali syndrome [3].
  - Beta-blockers, even cardioselective ones, may increase airway resistance and should be used with caution in elderly patients with obstructive lung disease [2].
  - Potassium-wasting diuretics may worsen carbon dioxide retention and hypokalemia in hypoventilating patients or those on corticosteroids or beta-agonists [2].

- **Monitoring Parameters:**
  - Monitor serum calcium and renal function (creatinine) at baseline and regularly during therapy, especially when combining calcium, vitamin D, and thiazide diuretics [3].
  - Increase frequency of calcium monitoring if the patient is on any additional agents that raise calcium levels [1].
  - Monitor ECG if digoxin and calcium are used together [1].
  - Monitor blood pressure closely if calcium is used with calcium channel blockers [1].
  - For patients with COPD on potassium-wasting diuretics, monitor electrolytes (potassium, calcium, bicarbonate) and consider potassium supplementation or potassium-sparing agents if indicated [2].

- **Summary of Recommendations:**
  - Avoid unnecessary combination of calcium supplements, vitamin D, and thiazide diuretics in elderly hypertensive patients.
  - If combination therapy is necessary, implement frequent laboratory monitoring and adjust medications promptly if hypercalcemia or renal dysfunction develops.
  - Be vigilant for symptoms of hypercalcemia (nausea, vomiting, confusion, muscle weakness) and metabolic alkalosis in elderly patients on these regimens [3].
