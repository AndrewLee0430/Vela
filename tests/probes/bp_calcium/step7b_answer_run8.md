## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and increased risk of hypercalcemia or other adverse effects. Specific combinations, such as calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers or digoxin, require close monitoring or should generally be avoided due to risks of reduced drug efficacy or synergistic arrhythmias, respectively [1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Chloride and Digoxin**: Avoid concomitant use due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration [1].
- **Calcium Chloride and Calcium Channel Blockers**: Avoid concomitant use as calcium chloride may reduce the response to calcium channel blockers. If used together, monitor blood pressure closely during administration [1].
- **Calcium Chloride and Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk**: When calcium chloride is used with other agents that increase calcium levels (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium), increase the frequency of calcium concentration monitoring to detect and manage hypercalcemia early [1].
- **Hydrochlorothiazide (a thiazide diuretic)**: Thiazide diuretics can increase calcium levels, further raising the risk of hypercalcemia when combined with calcium supplements or calcium chloride [2]. Monitor serum calcium regularly, especially in elderly patients who may have reduced renal function.
- **Lithium**: Avoid combining lithium with thiazide diuretics due to greatly increased risk of lithium toxicity [2].
- **Monitoring Parameters**:
  - **Serum Calcium**: Check at baseline and increase monitoring frequency if combining calcium with thiazide diuretics or other hypercalcemia-risk drugs [1].
  - **ECG**: Monitor closely if calcium chloride and digoxin are used together [1].
  - **Blood Pressure**: Monitor closely if calcium chloride and calcium channel blockers are used together [1].
- **General Considerations**: Elderly patients are at higher risk for drug interactions and adverse effects due to comorbidities and altered pharmacokinetics. Individualize therapy and monitor closely.

The provided context does not address all BP medications or all forms of calcium supplementation, and does not include data specific to elderly populations or long-term outcomes. Clinical judgment and additional sources may be needed for comprehensive management.
