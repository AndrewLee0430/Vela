## Summary

Elderly patients can generally take blood pressure medications (BP meds) with calcium, but caution is required due to the potential for clinically significant drug-drug and drug-food interactions involving calcium channel blockers (CCBs) and calcium supplements or foods high in calcium[1]. The main concern is that calcium can interfere with the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of CCBs, potentially reducing their antihypertensive effect[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Calcium channel blockers are commonly used in elderly patients for hypertension, angina, and arrhythmias, often in combination with other therapies[1].
- Calcium supplements or high-calcium foods may decrease the effectiveness of CCBs by antagonizing their action, as calcium can counteract the vasodilatory effect of these drugs[1].
- Clinicians should monitor blood pressure closely when elderly patients are taking both BP medications (especially CCBs) and calcium supplements or high-calcium foods[1].
- There is an increased risk of drug-drug and drug-food interactions in elderly patients due to polypharmacy and age-related changes in drug metabolism[1].
- The provided context does not specify exact thresholds for calcium intake or monitoring frequency, nor does it detail interactions with other classes of BP medications.
- If a reduction in antihypertensive efficacy is suspected, consider adjusting the dose of the CCB or reviewing the necessity and dosage of calcium supplementation[1].
- Regular review of all medications and supplements in elderly patients is recommended to minimize interaction risks[1].
- Evidence is from 2005 and may not reflect the most current clinical guidelines or newer antihypertensive agents.
