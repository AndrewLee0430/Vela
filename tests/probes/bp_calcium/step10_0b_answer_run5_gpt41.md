## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications together with calcium supplementation, specifically calcium citrate, as current evidence shows favorable adherence and safety in this population, with no serious adverse reactions reported when used together [1]. Notably, calcium citrate supplementation was associated with a decrease in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure (mean change -2.8 mmHg and -2.1 mmHg, respectively) in elderly osteopenic subjects [1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- In a prospective study of elderly osteopenic subjects (mean age 70), daily supplementation with 500 mg calcium citrate for up to one year was well tolerated, with only 3.9% reporting adverse reactions, primarily mild gastrointestinal symptoms; no serious adverse reactions were observed [1].
- Both systolic and diastolic blood pressure decreased modestly during calcium citrate supplementation (mean change -2.8 ± 13.9 mmHg and -2.1 ± 10.4 mmHg, respectively; p = 0.0102 and p = 0.0116) [1].
- There is no evidence from the provided sources of clinically significant drug interactions or contraindications between calcium supplementation and antihypertensive medications in the elderly [1], but the study population was primarily Caucasian and osteopenic, so applicability to other groups is not established.
- Adherence to calcium citrate was generally high, and adverse events were more common in those with lower adherence (<80%) [1].
- No specific monitoring parameters or thresholds for calcium supplementation in combination with BP medications are provided in the available evidence. However, routine monitoring for gastrointestinal tolerance and periodic assessment of blood pressure and serum calcium may be prudent in elderly patients.
- The evidence does not address potential interactions with specific classes of antihypertensive drugs (e.g., thiazides, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors), nor does it cover patients with advanced renal impairment or those at risk for hypercalcemia.
- If the patient is on thiazide diuretics, be aware that thiazides can increase serum calcium; monitor for hypercalcemia if clinically indicated (not directly addressed in the provided sources).
- There is no information in the provided context regarding patients with chronic kidney disease, severe cardiac disease, or other metabolic disturbances—caution is advised in these populations due to the lack of direct evidence.
