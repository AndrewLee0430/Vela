## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required, especially with calcium channel blockers. Concomitant use of calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers may reduce the effectiveness of the BP medication, and close blood pressure monitoring is recommended if co-administration is unavoidable[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers, as calcium may reduce the antihypertensive response of these medications[1].
- If co-administration cannot be avoided, monitor blood pressure closely during administration of calcium chloride injection[1].
- Calcium chloride should also be avoided with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased risk of digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia; if unavoidable, monitor ECG closely[1].
- For patients on other drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium), increase the frequency of serum calcium monitoring[1].
- The provided context does not specify interactions with all classes of BP medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers), so caution should be individualized based on the specific BP medication class.
- No specific age-related contraindications are mentioned, but elderly patients may be at higher risk for drug interactions and adverse effects, so monitoring should be especially diligent.
