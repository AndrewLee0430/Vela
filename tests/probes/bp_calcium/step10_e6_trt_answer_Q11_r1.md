## Summary

There is no evidence of clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions between calcium antagonists (calcium channel blockers) and ACE inhibitors such as lisinopril. These agents have additive antihypertensive effects, but pharmacokinetic interactions are unlikely according to available data[2].

---

## Clinical Notes

- The combination of calcium channel blockers and ACE inhibitors like lisinopril is generally considered safe, with additive blood pressure-lowering effects[2].
- No pharmacokinetic interaction is expected between calcium channel blockers and ACE inhibitors, so no dose adjustment is typically required when these drugs are used together[2].
- Monitor blood pressure regularly to assess for excessive hypotension due to the additive effect.
- There is no mention in the provided context of increased risk for hyperkalemia, renal dysfunction, or other specific adverse effects unique to this combination[2].
- If the patient has other comorbidities (e.g., renal impairment, heart failure), follow standard monitoring protocols for ACE inhibitor therapy (e.g., check renal function and electrolytes at baseline and periodically thereafter)[1].
- The evidence cited is from 1993 and may not reflect the most recent clinical data[2].
