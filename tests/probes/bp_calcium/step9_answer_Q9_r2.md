## Summary  
In elderly individuals without cardiovascular disease, daily low-dose aspirin (75-100 mg) does not significantly affect blood pressure when taken alongside blood pressure medications. Aspirin use showed no meaningful change in systolic or diastolic blood pressure compared to placebo over approximately 4.7 years, regardless of baseline blood pressure or antihypertensive treatment status [2]. This suggests aspirin can be safely combined with blood pressure medications in this population without concern for adverse blood pressure effects.

## Clinical Notes  
- Aspirin at low doses (75-100 mg daily) does not significantly alter blood pressure in elderly patients on antihypertensive therapy [2].  
- No difference in incident treated hypertension rates was observed between aspirin and placebo groups, indicating aspirin does not increase the risk of developing hypertension in this context [2].  
- The polypill containing aspirin plus blood pressure-lowering drugs showed a reduction in cardiovascular events without significant adverse blood pressure effects, supporting combined use in primary prevention for those with elevated cardiovascular risk [1].  
- Monitor blood pressure routinely as per standard care; no additional aspirin-specific BP monitoring is indicated based on current evidence.  
- Consider contraindications for aspirin use such as bleeding risk, allergy, or other individual factors not detailed in the provided data.  
- Evidence is from community-dwelling older adults without cardiovascular disease, dementia, or significant disability, primarily from Australian and U.S. cohorts [2]; applicability to other populations should be considered cautiously.
