## Summary  
Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and risks. Calcium chloride should not be used concomitantly with calcium channel blockers as it may reduce their effectiveness, and close blood pressure monitoring is necessary if combined. Additionally, calcium chloride can increase the risk of hypercalcemia, especially when used with other drugs that raise calcium levels, necessitating frequent calcium monitoring [1]. Hydrochlorothiazide, a common BP medication, has interactions with lithium and NSAIDs that may affect electrolyte balance but no direct contraindication with calcium is noted [2].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Calcium Chloride and Calcium Channel Blockers:** Avoid concurrent use because calcium chloride may reduce the antihypertensive response of calcium channel blockers. If unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration [1].  
- **Risk of Hypercalcemia:** Calcium chloride can cause hypercalcemia, which increases the risk of digoxin toxicity and other complications. In elderly patients, who may be more vulnerable, monitor serum calcium levels frequently if calcium chloride is used with other hypercalcemia-inducing drugs (e.g., thiazide diuretics, Vitamin D) [1].  
- **Digoxin Interaction:** Avoid combining calcium chloride with digoxin due to synergistic arrhythmia risk and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. ECG monitoring is essential if combined use cannot be avoided [1].  
- **Hydrochlorothiazide Considerations:** This thiazide diuretic may require dose adjustments when used with antidiabetic drugs and can potentiate orthostatic hypotension with alcohol or narcotics. It also interacts with lithium (increased lithium toxicity risk) and NSAIDs (reduced diuretic effect), but no direct contraindication with calcium supplements or calcium chloride is reported [2].  
- **Monitoring Recommendations:**  
  - Check serum calcium levels regularly when calcium chloride is administered, especially in elderly patients or those on other hypercalcemia-risk drugs.  
  - Monitor blood pressure closely if calcium chloride is given with calcium channel blockers.  
  - ECG monitoring is advised if calcium chloride is used with digoxin.  
  - Monitor electrolytes and renal function in elderly patients on hydrochlorothiazide, particularly if combined with lithium or NSAIDs.  

The provided documents do not specifically address elderly patients as a separate group, but the above precautions apply generally and are especially relevant in older adults due to their increased susceptibility to adverse effects and drug interactions.
