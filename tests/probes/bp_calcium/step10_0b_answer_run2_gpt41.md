## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure medications with calcium, but caution is required, especially with thiazide diuretics. Concurrent use of thiazide diuretics and calcium (with or without vitamin D) increases the risk of hypercalcemia, acute kidney injury, and metabolic alkalosis—a syndrome known as Calcium Alkali Thiazide Syndrome (CATS) [3]. The mechanism involves thiazide-induced decreased renal calcium excretion, which, when combined with high calcium intake, can lead to dangerous elevations in serum calcium [3].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Thiazide diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone) are commonly used antihypertensives in the elderly, but they reduce renal calcium excretion and can potentiate hypercalcemia when combined with calcium and vitamin D supplements [3].
- The triad of hypercalcemia, acute kidney injury, and metabolic alkalosis (CATS) is a serious risk when thiazides are combined with high calcium/vitamin D intake [3]. Symptoms may include nausea, vomiting, confusion, neuromuscular symptoms, and renal dysfunction.
- Elderly patients, especially those with reduced renal function or a history of parathyroid disease, are at higher risk for CATS [3].
- Monitoring:
  - Check serum calcium, creatinine, and bicarbonate at baseline and periodically (e.g., every 1–3 months) in patients on thiazides with calcium/vitamin D supplements [3].
  - Discontinue thiazide or reduce calcium/vitamin D intake if serum calcium exceeds the upper limit of normal (typically >10.8 mg/dL) or if there is evidence of acute kidney injury [3].
- Thiazide diuretics should be used with caution in patients with chronic kidney disease or those already at risk for hypercalcemia [3].
- Other antihypertensive agents (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs) do not have the same risk of hypercalcemia and may be preferred in patients requiring calcium supplementation [1].
- Always review all medications and supplements in elderly patients to avoid unintentional additive effects leading to CATS [3].
- If thiazide diuretics are necessary, use the lowest effective dose and educate patients about the symptoms of hypercalcemia [3].

There is insufficient context regarding the safety of other classes of antihypertensives with calcium; the provided evidence specifically addresses thiazide diuretics.
