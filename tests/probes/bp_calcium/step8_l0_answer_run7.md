## Summary  
Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications including calcium channel blockers, but concomitant use with calcium chloride requires caution. Calcium chloride may reduce the effectiveness of calcium channel blockers, so if used together, blood pressure should be closely monitored during administration to avoid suboptimal BP control or adverse effects [1].

## Clinical Notes  
- Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers if possible due to potential reduction in drug response [1].  
- If unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during calcium chloride administration to detect any changes in BP control [1].  
- Elderly patients often have altered pharmacodynamics and may be more sensitive to changes in calcium levels; careful monitoring is essential.  
- No direct contraindication to BP medications in elderly patients from the provided data, but the interaction with calcium chloride is a key consideration.  
- Additional monitoring of calcium concentrations may be warranted if patients are on other drugs that increase hypercalcemia risk alongside calcium chloride [1].  
- The provided information does not specify other classes of BP medications beyond calcium channel blockers; thus, safety with other BP meds cannot be concluded from this context.
