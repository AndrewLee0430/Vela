## Summary  
Lisinopril, an ACE inhibitor, is effective in lowering blood pressure and providing renal protection in diabetic patients, with additional benefits in slowing retinopathy progression and potentially improving neurological function. Calcium channel blockers, while also antihypertensive, appear less renoprotective than lisinopril in diabetic nephropathy. Both drug classes are commonly used for hypertension, but lisinopril has a more pronounced benefit on diabetic complications [2]. However, recent concerns have emerged about nitrosamine contamination in calcium channel blockers (e.g., amlodipine) and ACE inhibitors (e.g., lisinopril), which may be linked to increased cancer risks, including skin cancer and other malignancies [1].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety and Use**: Lisinopril is generally well tolerated and effective in diabetic patients with hypertension and nephropathy, including normotensive patients with microalbuminuria. It does not adversely affect glycemic control or lipid profiles [2]. Calcium channel blockers are effective antihypertensives but may offer less renal protection in diabetes compared to lisinopril [2].  
- **Contraindications**: Both lisinopril and calcium channel blockers should be used cautiously or avoided in patients with known hypersensitivity, history of angioedema (for lisinopril), or severe hypotension. Lisinopril is contraindicated in pregnancy.  
- **Monitoring**: For lisinopril, monitor renal function (e.g., serum creatinine, eGFR) and serum potassium at baseline and periodically during treatment, especially in diabetic patients or those with renal impairment. Blood pressure should be regularly monitored to avoid hypotension. For calcium channel blockers, monitor blood pressure and signs of peripheral edema or other adverse effects.  
- **Emerging Safety Concerns**: Recent data highlight nitrosamine contamination in both lisinopril and calcium channel blockers, which may increase the risk of various cancers, including skin cancer and gastrointestinal tumors [1]. This contamination issue warrants vigilance and may influence drug choice or require regulatory review.  
- **Clinical Decision**: When choosing between lisinopril and calcium channel blockers in diabetic hypertensive patients, lisinopril may be preferred for renal and microvascular protection, but clinicians should consider the potential risks related to nitrosamine contamination and monitor accordingly [1][2].  

Further data on the clinical impact of nitrosamine contamination and comparative safety in diverse populations are needed.
