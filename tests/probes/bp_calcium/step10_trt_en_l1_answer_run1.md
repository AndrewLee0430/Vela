## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections together with blood pressure medications that are calcium channel blockers, as this combination may reduce the effectiveness of the blood pressure medication and requires close monitoring if unavoidable[1]. The main mechanism is that calcium chloride can antagonize the effects of calcium channel blockers, potentially leading to reduced blood pressure control[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers (a common class of blood pressure medications) should be avoided because calcium chloride may reduce the response to these medications[1].
- If the combination is unavoidable, blood pressure should be monitored closely during administration of calcium chloride injection[1].
- Calcium chloride injection can also increase the risk of hypercalcemia, especially when used with other drugs that elevate calcium levels (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium)[1]. In such cases, increase the frequency of monitoring serum calcium concentrations.
- For patients on digoxin, avoid concurrent use with calcium chloride injection due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia; if unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration[1].
- The provided context does not specify interactions with other classes of blood pressure medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, ARBs), so caution should be exercised and further information sought for those combinations.
