## Summary

You should generally avoid taking potassium supplements with blood pressure (BP) pills, especially if your BP medication is a potassium-sparing diuretic or a drug that affects the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), such as ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), because this combination can increase your risk of dangerous hyperkalemia (high blood potassium) [1][2][3][4]. If potassium supplementation is considered necessary, your potassium levels must be closely monitored due to the risk of serious complications.

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Do NOT use potassium supplements with potassium-sparing diuretics** (e.g., amiloride, spironolactone, triamterene) because this can cause severe hyperkalemia [1][2][3].
- **Caution is required with RAAS inhibitors** (ACE inhibitors, ARBs like valsartan or losartan, aldosterone antagonists, or direct renin inhibitors like aliskiren). These drugs already increase potassium retention, so adding potassium supplements can further raise potassium to dangerous levels [1][2][4].
- **NSAIDs** (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) can also increase potassium when combined with potassium supplements or RAAS inhibitors, and may worsen kidney function [1][2][3][4].
- **If potassium supplementation is necessary** (e.g., for documented hypokalemia), frequent monitoring of serum potassium is essential. Check potassium at baseline, then at least every 1–2 weeks after starting or changing doses, and more often if there are risk factors for hyperkalemia (e.g., kidney impairment, elderly, diabetes) [1][2][3][4].
- **Monitor renal function** (serum creatinine, eGFR) regularly, especially in patients with heart failure, chronic kidney disease, or those on combinations of BP medications and potassium [2][4].
- **Symptoms of hyperkalemia** include muscle weakness, fatigue, palpitations, and in severe cases, cardiac arrhythmias. Immediate medical attention is required if these occur.
- **Potassium supplementation for hypertension**: Evidence from RCTs is limited and does not show a statistically significant blood pressure reduction in adults with hypertension, and the data are from studies before 2006 [5].

If you are unsure which BP medication you are taking, consult your healthcare provider before starting potassium supplements.
