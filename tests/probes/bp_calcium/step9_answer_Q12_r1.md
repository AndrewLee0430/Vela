## Summary  
Potassium supplementation combined with spironolactone increases plasma potassium levels and the risk of hyperkalemia due to potassium retention effects of spironolactone. Both potassium chloride and spironolactone raise plasma potassium concentrations significantly, with spironolactone showing a slightly larger effect on potassium levels. The combination requires careful potassium monitoring to avoid severe hyperkalemia, especially when used with other potassium-sparing agents or drugs affecting the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) [1][2][4].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety and Contraindications:**  
  - Avoid concomitant use of potassium chloride with potassium-sparing diuretics like spironolactone due to risk of severe hyperkalemia [1].  
  - Patients with impaired renal function, diabetes, proteinuria, or those on ACE inhibitors, ARBs, NSAIDs, or CYP3A inhibitors are at increased risk of hyperkalemia when using spironolactone [1][3].  
  - High dietary potassium intake can exacerbate hyperkalemia risk in patients on spironolactone and ACE inhibitors [4].  
- **Monitoring:**  
  - Monitor serum potassium closely when spironolactone is used, especially if combined with potassium supplements or RAAS inhibitors [1][3].  
  - For hypertensive patients on thiazides and spironolactone or potassium chloride, plasma potassium peaks 2-3 hours post-dose and tends to wane by 12 hours; twice daily dosing with monitoring is generally adequate [2].  
  - In healthy young women treated with spironolactone for acne, potassium monitoring rates have decreased, reflecting low hyperkalemia risk in this population, but monitoring remains important in higher-risk groups [5].  
- **Dose Adjustments:**  
  - Dose reduction of spironolactone may be necessary if hyperkalemia develops (potassium 5.5-5.9 mEq/L) [3].  
  - Avoid or adjust doses of interacting drugs that increase potassium retention (e.g., RAAS inhibitors, NSAIDs) [1][3].  
- **Patient Counseling:**  
  - Advise patients on dietary potassium intake, especially in populations with high consumption of potassium-rich foods like bananas [4].  
  - Educate about symptoms of hyperkalemia and the importance of adherence to monitoring schedules.
