## Summary  
Taking potassium supplements or potassium-containing salt substitutes together with blood pressure (BP) medications such as angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) or ACE inhibitors can increase the risk of hyperkalemia (high potassium levels in the blood). This is because these BP medications reduce aldosterone production, leading to potassium retention. Therefore, potassium should be used cautiously and under close medical supervision when combined with these drugs to avoid dangerous potassium elevations [1][2][4].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Avoid combining potassium supplements with potassium-sparing diuretics and RAAS inhibitors (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone, eplerenone, aliskiren)** due to a high risk of severe hyperkalemia [1][2][3][4].  
- **Monitor serum potassium levels closely** if potassium supplementation is necessary while on these BP medications. Frequent potassium checks are recommended to detect hyperkalemia early [1][2][3][4].  
- **NSAIDs can also increase potassium levels and impair renal function** when used with these BP drugs, further increasing hyperkalemia risk. Renal function and potassium should be monitored if NSAIDs are used concomitantly [1][2][4].  
- **Dual blockade of the renin-angiotensin system (e.g., ACE inhibitor plus ARB) increases risks of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and renal impairment** and should generally be avoided [2][4].  
- **Patients with renal impairment, elderly, or volume-depleted are at higher risk** for hyperkalemia and renal dysfunction when combining potassium with these BP medications [2][4].  
- **Lithium toxicity risk increases when combined with these drugs and potassium-sparing diuretics**; lithium levels should be monitored if co-administered [2][3][4].  

In summary, potassium supplementation with BP pills that affect the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system requires careful monitoring and is often contraindicated without medical supervision due to the risk of hyperkalemia and renal complications.
