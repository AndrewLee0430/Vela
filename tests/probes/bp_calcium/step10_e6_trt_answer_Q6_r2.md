## Summary

Elderly patients can generally take vitamin D with thiazide diuretics ("water pills"), but there is a small risk of hypercalcemia, especially in those with compromised renal function or hyperparathyroidism. Evidence shows that vitamin D supplementation (up to 4000 IU daily) in thiazide users increases serum calcium slightly, but the frequency of clinically significant hypercalcemia is low in otherwise healthy individuals [1], [4]. The risk may be higher in elderly patients with additional risk factors.

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Safety Warnings & Contraindications:**  
  - Caution is advised in elderly patients with impaired renal function or hyperparathyroidism, as the combination of thiazide diuretics and vitamin D may increase the risk of hypercalcemia [4].
  - Thiazide diuretics decrease urinary calcium excretion, and vitamin D increases intestinal calcium absorption, which together can raise serum calcium levels [1], [4].
  - In patients with hypoparathyroidism, thiazides can cause a more pronounced increase in serum calcium, especially when pharmacologic doses of vitamin D are used [3].

- **Monitoring Parameters:**  
  - **Serum calcium:** Check at baseline and periodically during combined therapy, especially after starting or increasing vitamin D supplementation. Consider monitoring every 1–3 months in high-risk elderly patients.
  - **Renal function (eGFR):** Assess at baseline and monitor regularly, as impaired renal function increases the risk of hypercalcemia and related complications [4].
  - **Signs of hypercalcemia:** Monitor for symptoms such as confusion, muscle weakness, constipation, or arrhythmias.

- **When NOT to Use:**  
  - Avoid the combination in elderly patients with known hypercalcemia, severe renal impairment, or uncontrolled hyperparathyroidism [4].
  - Use caution or avoid in those with a history of calcium kidney stones.

- **Drug Interactions:**  
  - The risk of hypercalcemia is particularly increased when thiazide diuretics are combined with both vitamin D and calcium supplements [4].

- **Evidence Limitations:**  
  - Most studies are small, observational, or not focused exclusively on the elderly population [1], [4], [5]. Data on frail or institutionalized elderly are limited.

In summary, vitamin D can be used with thiazide diuretics in the elderly with appropriate monitoring, but extra caution is warranted in those with renal impairment or other risk factors for hypercalcemia.
