## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to the risk of hypercalcemia, particularly when thiazide diuretics are used concurrently with calcium and vitamin D supplements. This combination can lead to severe hypercalcemia, acute kidney injury, and metabolic alkalosis, known as Calcium Alkali Thiazide Syndrome (CATS) [3]. The mechanism involves thiazide-induced decreased renal calcium excretion, potentiated by supplemental calcium and vitamin D, increasing the risk of dangerous calcium elevations [3].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Key Drug Interactions and Risks:**
  - Thiazide diuretics combined with calcium and vitamin D supplements can significantly increase the risk of hypercalcemia, especially in elderly patients or those with reduced renal function [3].
  - Hypercalcemia may present with symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, confusion, neuromuscular symptoms, and can progress to acute kidney injury and metabolic alkalosis (CATS) [3].
  - Calcium chloride should be avoided with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, close ECG monitoring is required [1].
  - Calcium chloride may reduce the efficacy of calcium channel blockers; if used together, monitor blood pressure closely [1].
  - Other drugs that increase hypercalcemia risk (e.g., calcipotriene, estrogen, lithium, parathyroid hormone, teriparatide, thiazide diuretics, vitamin A, vitamin D) require increased frequency of calcium monitoring when used with calcium chloride [1].

- **Contraindications and Cautions:**
  - Avoid thiazide diuretics in patients with a history of hypercalcemia or those on high-dose calcium/vitamin D supplementation unless absolutely necessary [3].
  - Use beta-blockers with caution in patients with obstructive lung disease (e.g., COPD), as they may increase airway resistance [2].
  - Potassium-wasting diuretics may worsen carbon dioxide retention and hypokalemia in COPD patients, especially if also on corticosteroids or beta-agonists [2].

- **Monitoring Recommendations:**
  - Monitor serum calcium at baseline and regularly (frequency individualized, but at least every few weeks initially, then periodically) in patients on calcium supplements and thiazide diuretics or other hypercalcemia-risk drugs [1], [3].
  - Monitor renal function (serum creatinine, eGFR) at baseline and periodically, especially in elderly or those with pre-existing kidney disease [3].
  - In patients with COPD on potassium-wasting diuretics, monitor serum potassium and acid-base status closely; consider potassium supplementation or potassium-sparing agents if hypokalemia develops [2].
  - For patients on digoxin and calcium, monitor ECG continuously during administration and check for signs of digoxin toxicity [1].

- **When NOT to Use:**
  - Do not use calcium chloride with digoxin unless absolutely necessary [1].
  - Avoid combining thiazide diuretics with high-dose calcium and vitamin D supplements in elderly patients or those with impaired renal function [3].
