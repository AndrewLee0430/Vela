## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions. Concomitant use of calcium (specifically intravenous calcium chloride) with calcium channel blockers may reduce the effectiveness of the BP medication, and with digoxin, it may increase the risk of arrhythmias and digoxin toxicity [1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection together with calcium channel blockers if possible, as calcium may reduce the antihypertensive effect of these medications. If co-administration is unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration [1].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride injection and digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity, especially if hypercalcemia develops. If unavoidable, closely monitor ECG during administration [1].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** If calcium is given with drugs that increase serum calcium (e.g., calcipotriene, estrogen, lithium, parathyroid hormone, teriparatide, thiazide diuretics, Vitamin A, Vitamin D), increase the frequency of serum calcium monitoring [1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - Monitor blood pressure closely if calcium is used with calcium channel blockers.
  - Monitor ECG if calcium is used with digoxin.
  - Monitor serum calcium concentrations more frequently if there is risk of hypercalcemia.
