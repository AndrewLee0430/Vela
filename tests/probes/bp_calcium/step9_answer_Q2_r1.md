## Summary  
Taking potassium supplements or potassium-containing salt substitutes together with blood pressure (BP) medications such as angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) or ACE inhibitors can increase the risk of hyperkalemia (high serum potassium). This is because these BP drugs reduce aldosterone production, leading to potassium retention. Therefore, potassium should be used cautiously and only under close medical supervision with frequent potassium monitoring when combined with these medications [1][2][4].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Avoid concomitant use of potassium supplements with potassium-sparing diuretics** (e.g., amiloride, spironolactone) as this can cause severe hyperkalemia [1][3].  
- **Patients on renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors** (ACE inhibitors, ARBs like valsartan or losartan) should have serum potassium closely monitored if potassium supplements are used concurrently, due to increased potassium retention risk [1][2][4].  
- **Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)** can also increase potassium levels and impair renal function when combined with BP medications, so potassium levels and renal function should be monitored if these drugs are used together [1][2][3][4].  
- **Monitoring recommendations:**  
  - Check baseline serum potassium before starting potassium supplements with BP meds.  
  - Monitor potassium frequently during treatment, especially if using potassium-sparing diuretics or RAAS inhibitors.  
  - Monitor renal function periodically, particularly if NSAIDs are also used.  
- **Contraindications:** Avoid potassium supplements in patients with hyperkalemia or severe renal impairment.  
- **If potassium supplementation is necessary due to hypokalemia, use with caution and under strict monitoring** to avoid dangerous potassium elevation [3].  

In summary, potassium supplementation with BP medications that affect the RAAS system requires careful monitoring to prevent hyperkalemia and related complications.
