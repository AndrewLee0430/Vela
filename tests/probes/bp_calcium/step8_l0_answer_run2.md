## Summary  
Elderly patients can take blood pressure medications along with calcium supplements, but caution is needed, especially if thiazide diuretics are involved. Thiazides can increase calcium levels and, when combined with calcium and vitamin D supplements, may lead to Calcium Alkali Thiazide Syndrome (CATS), characterized by hypercalcemia, metabolic alkalosis, and acute kidney injury [3]. Monitoring calcium levels and kidney function is essential to avoid this complication.

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety and Risks:**  
  - Thiazide diuretics increase calcium reabsorption in the kidneys, which can elevate serum calcium levels. When combined with calcium and vitamin D supplements, this may precipitate hypercalcemia and Calcium Alkali Thiazide Syndrome (CATS), especially in elderly patients or those with prior parathyroid issues [3].  
  - Elderly patients on thiazides and calcium supplements should be monitored closely for symptoms of hypercalcemia such as nausea, vomiting, confusion, and neuromuscular symptoms.  
  - Thiazides can cause electrolyte imbalances, including hypokalemia, which may be worsened by corticosteroids or beta-agonists often used in elderly patients with respiratory conditions [2].  
- **Contraindications and Cautions:**  
  - Avoid combining thiazide diuretics with lithium due to increased risk of lithium toxicity [1].  
  - Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) may reduce the efficacy of thiazide diuretics and should be used cautiously [1].  
  - In patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), thiazides are preferred over beta-blockers for hypertension, but potassium levels should be monitored closely due to risk of hypokalemia [2].  
- **Monitoring Parameters:**  
  - Check serum calcium and creatinine at baseline and periodically during therapy when thiazides are combined with calcium and vitamin D supplements.  
  - Monitor for signs of hypercalcemia and acute kidney injury; discontinue or adjust therapy if calcium rises significantly or renal function deteriorates [3].  
  - Monitor electrolytes, especially potassium, in patients on thiazides, corticosteroids, or beta-agonists [2].  
  - Adjust antihypertensive and supplement doses based on laboratory results and clinical status.  

In summary, elderly patients can take blood pressure medications with calcium supplements, but thiazide diuretics require careful monitoring to prevent serious complications like CATS.
