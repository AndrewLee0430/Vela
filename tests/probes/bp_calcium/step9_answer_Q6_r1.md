## Summary  
Elderly patients can take vitamin D supplements with thiazide diuretics ("water pills"), but there is an increased risk of hypercalcemia, especially in those with compromised renal function, hyperparathyroidism, or chronic kidney disease. Vitamin D supplementation up to 4000 IU daily in thiazide users modestly raises serum calcium but rarely causes overt hypercalcemia. However, caution and monitoring are advised due to potential drug interactions and increased calcium levels [1][2][5].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety warnings:**  
  - Thiazide diuretics reduce urinary calcium excretion, which combined with vitamin D supplementation can increase serum calcium levels and risk of hypercalcemia, particularly in elderly patients or those with renal impairment or hyperparathyroidism [1][2][5].  
  - Topical vitamin D3 analogs combined with thiazides also increase hypercalcemia risk, especially in patients aged ≥70 years or with chronic kidney disease [5].  
- **Contraindications:**  
  - Avoid or use with caution in patients with pre-existing hypercalcemia, severe renal impairment, or hyperparathyroidism.  
  - Use caution if patients are on lithium, corticosteroids, or NSAIDs, as these can interact with thiazides and affect electrolyte balance or diuretic efficacy [3].  
- **Monitoring:**  
  - Check serum calcium levels at baseline and periodically during combined vitamin D and thiazide therapy, especially in elderly or those with risk factors.  
  - Monitor renal function (e.g., eGFR) before and during treatment; dose adjustments or discontinuation may be needed if renal function declines.  
  - Observe for symptoms of hypercalcemia (e.g., weakness, confusion, polyuria).  
- **Dosing considerations:**  
  - Vitamin D doses up to 4000 IU daily have been studied without frequent hypercalcemia, but individual risk varies [1].  
- **Additional notes:**  
  - The evidence is primarily from studies in Black populations and elderly patients; applicability to other ethnic groups or younger adults may vary [1][4].  
  - Larger, high-quality studies are needed to fully clarify the interaction and optimal monitoring strategies [2][4].
