## Summary  
Elderly patients can generally take blood pressure (BP) medications alongside calcium supplements such as calcium citrate, as no direct contraindications are reported in the provided data. Calcium citrate supplementation in elderly osteopenic subjects showed good safety and tolerability, with a noted modest decrease in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure (-2.8 mmHg and -2.1 mmHg respectively), suggesting a potential mild beneficial effect on BP control [1]. However, specific interactions between calcium supplements and various classes of antihypertensive drugs were not detailed in the provided documents.

## Clinical Notes  
- Calcium citrate supplementation in elderly patients (mean age 70) was well tolerated with low adverse reaction rates (3.9%), mostly gastrointestinal, and no serious adverse events reported [1].  
- Calcium supplementation may modestly lower blood pressure, which could be beneficial in hypertensive elderly patients [1].  
- No explicit contraindications or safety warnings were noted regarding the concurrent use of calcium supplements with antihypertensive medications in the elderly population studied [1].  
- In patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) on antihypertensive therapy, special caution is advised with certain BP meds (e.g., beta-blockers and potassium-wasting diuretics) due to risks of airway resistance and electrolyte disturbances, but calcium supplements were not implicated in these concerns [3].  
- Monitoring should focus on adherence and gastrointestinal tolerance of calcium supplements, and routine BP monitoring remains essential to assess the combined effect of calcium and antihypertensive drugs [1].  
- The evidence does not specify interactions with specific BP drug classes; therefore, clinical judgment and monitoring for any unexpected effects remain important.  
- Additional data would be needed to clarify interactions between calcium supplements and specific antihypertensive agents or in patients with complex comorbidities.  

In summary, calcium citrate can be safely used in elderly patients taking BP medications, with potential mild BP-lowering effects, but clinical monitoring is advised to ensure safety and efficacy [1][3].
