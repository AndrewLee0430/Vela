## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injection together with blood pressure medications known as calcium channel blockers, as concomitant use may reduce the effectiveness of the blood pressure medication and requires close blood pressure monitoring if unavoidable[1]. Other blood pressure medications are not specifically addressed in the provided context, so further information is needed for those classes.

## Clinical Notes

- Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers should be avoided because calcium chloride may reduce the response to calcium channel blockers[1].
- If co-administration is unavoidable, blood pressure should be monitored closely during administration of calcium chloride injection[1].
- The context does not provide information about interactions between calcium chloride and other classes of blood pressure medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics other than thiazides).
- For patients taking drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (including thiazide diuretics), increase the frequency of monitoring serum calcium concentrations[1].
- There is no specific guidance in the provided context regarding oral calcium supplements or non-injectable forms.
- Always assess for other potential drug interactions and monitor for signs of hypercalcemia, especially in elderly patients who may have reduced renal function or polypharmacy[1].
