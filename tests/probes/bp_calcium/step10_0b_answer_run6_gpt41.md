## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and increased risk of hypercalcemia. Concomitant use of calcium (such as calcium chloride) with certain antihypertensive drugs—especially calcium channel blockers and thiazide diuretics—requires close monitoring and may necessitate dose adjustments or avoidance in some cases [1], [2].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium and Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use of calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers may reduce the antihypertensive effect of the calcium channel blocker. If use together is unavoidable, blood pressure should be monitored closely during administration [1].
- **Calcium and Thiazide Diuretics:** Thiazide diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide) can increase serum calcium levels. When used with calcium supplements or calcium chloride, there is a higher risk of hypercalcemia. Increased frequency of calcium concentration monitoring is recommended in these patients [1], [2].
- **Other Drug Interactions:** Calcium chloride should be avoided with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, ECG monitoring is required [1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - Monitor serum calcium levels frequently when calcium is combined with thiazide diuretics or other drugs that increase calcium (e.g., vitamin D, lithium, parathyroid hormone analogs) [1].
  - Monitor blood pressure closely if combining calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers [1].
  - Monitor ECG if combining calcium chloride with digoxin [1].
- **Elderly Considerations:** Elderly patients are at increased risk for adverse effects such as orthostatic hypotension (especially with antihypertensives) and electrolyte disturbances. Careful dose titration and monitoring are essential [2].
- **Contraindications and Cautions:** Avoid combining calcium chloride with digoxin or calcium channel blockers unless absolutely necessary. Avoid or use with caution in patients at risk for hypercalcemia [1].

The provided context does not specify all possible antihypertensive classes or address all comorbidities common in the elderly, so further clinical judgment and individualized assessment are required.
