## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injection concomitantly with calcium channel blockers, as calcium may reduce the response to these antihypertensive medications. If concomitant use is unavoidable, close blood pressure monitoring is required during administration. There is no specific information provided about oral calcium supplements and other classes of blood pressure medications in the supplied context[1].

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers (such as amlodipine, verapamil, diltiazem) may reduce the antihypertensive effect of the calcium channel blocker. Avoid this combination if possible. If unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration of calcium chloride injection[1].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased risk of digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration[1].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** If calcium chloride injection is used with drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., calcipotriene, estrogen, lithium, parathyroid hormone, teriparatide, thiazide diuretics, Vitamin A, and Vitamin D), increase the frequency of monitoring serum calcium concentrations[1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** For patients receiving calcium chloride injection with any of the above medications, monitor blood pressure, ECG (if on digoxin), and serum calcium levels more frequently as clinically indicated[1].
- **Evidence Gaps:** The provided context does not address oral calcium supplements or interactions with other classes of blood pressure medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics other than thiazides). No specific recommendations for elderly patients outside of these drug interactions are given.

If considering calcium supplementation in elderly patients on blood pressure medications, assess the specific antihypertensive class and monitor for potential drug interactions and hypercalcemia.
