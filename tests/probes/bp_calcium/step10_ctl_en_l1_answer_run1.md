## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and increased risk of hypercalcemia, especially with certain drug classes such as thiazide diuretics and calcium channel blockers. Calcium chloride can increase the risk of hypercalcemia when combined with thiazide diuretics or other drugs that raise calcium levels, and it may also reduce the effectiveness of calcium channel blockers or increase the risk of arrhythmias with digoxin [2][3].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Thiazide Diuretics:** Concomitant use of calcium (e.g., calcium chloride) and thiazide diuretics increases the risk of hypercalcemia. Monitor serum calcium concentrations more frequently in patients on both agents [2]. Thiazides should be discontinued before parathyroid function tests [1].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Calcium chloride may reduce the antihypertensive effect of calcium channel blockers. If co-administration is unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration [2][3].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid using calcium chloride with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration [2].
- **Other Drug Interactions:** Calcium chloride may interact with drugs that also increase serum calcium (e.g., vitamin D, parathyroid hormone, lithium), requiring increased frequency of calcium monitoring [2].
- **Monitoring:** For elderly patients on BP medications and calcium, monitor serum calcium levels regularly (frequency should be individualized based on risk factors and concomitant medications). Monitor for signs of hypercalcemia (e.g., confusion, muscle weakness, arrhythmias).
- **General Precautions:** Elderly patients are at higher risk for adverse drug interactions due to polypharmacy and age-related changes in renal function. Dose adjustments and close monitoring are recommended.
