## Summary

Elderly patients can generally take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium supplements, including calcium citrate, as no direct contraindication or major interaction is reported in the provided evidence. However, hydrochlorothiazide (a common BP medication) may have its absorption reduced by certain agents, and calcium supplementation in elderly osteopenic patients was shown to be safe and well-tolerated, with no serious adverse reactions reported[1], [2].

---

## Clinical Notes

- No direct drug-drug interaction between calcium citrate and BP medications (including hydrochlorothiazide) is described in the provided sources[1], [2].
- Hydrochlorothiazide absorption can be reduced by cholestyramine and colestipol resins, but not by calcium citrate per the available evidence[1].
- Hydrochlorothiazide may interact with other drugs, including lithium (risk of toxicity), non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (reduced antihypertensive effect), and antidiabetic agents (may require dose adjustment)[1].
- In elderly osteopenic subjects, daily 500 mg calcium citrate supplementation for up to one year was associated with good adherence and a low rate of adverse reactions (3.9%, mostly mild gastrointestinal symptoms), with no serious adverse events reported[2].
- Blood pressure decreased slightly in subjects taking calcium citrate (mean systolic change -2.8 mmHg, diastolic -2.1 mmHg; both p < 0.02)[2].
- Monitor for gastrointestinal symptoms when starting calcium citrate in elderly patients, and reassess adherence and tolerability periodically[2].
- No specific laboratory or monitoring parameters for calcium-BP medication coadministration are provided in the cited sources.
- If using hydrochlorothiazide, monitor for potential interactions with other drugs as listed above, and consider periodic electrolyte and renal function assessment as per standard practice[1].
