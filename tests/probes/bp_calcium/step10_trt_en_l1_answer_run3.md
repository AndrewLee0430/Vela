## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections concomitantly with calcium channel blockers, as calcium chloride may reduce the response to these antihypertensive medications. If concomitant use is unavoidable, close blood pressure monitoring is required during administration. There is no information in the provided context about oral calcium supplements or other classes of blood pressure medications in elderly patients[1].

## Clinical Notes

- Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers (such as amlodipine, verapamil, diltiazem) is not recommended because calcium chloride may reduce the antihypertensive effect of these drugs[1].
- If co-administration cannot be avoided, blood pressure should be monitored closely during calcium chloride injection[1].
- The provided context does not address oral calcium supplements or their interaction with blood pressure medications, nor does it discuss other antihypertensive drug classes (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics).
- There is no specific information in the provided sources regarding elderly-specific risks or recommendations for oral calcium and blood pressure medication co-administration.
- For patients taking drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (such as thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, or others listed), calcium levels should be monitored more frequently if calcium chloride injection is used[1].
- No guidance is provided regarding the safety of combining oral calcium preparations with blood pressure medications in elderly patients[1][2]. Additional evidence would be needed for a comprehensive answer in this population.
