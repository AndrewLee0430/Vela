## Summary  
In elderly patients using diuretics, magnesium disturbances are a significant concern due to the risk of electrolyte imbalance. Diuretics, especially loop diuretics, can cause magnesium depletion, which may contribute to complications such as cognitive impairment and falls. Careful monitoring and management of magnesium levels are essential to avoid these adverse effects in this vulnerable population [1].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety warnings:** Elderly patients on diuretics are at increased risk for electrolyte imbalances, including hypomagnesemia, which can exacerbate frailty, cognitive impairment, and increase fall risk.  
- **Contraindications:** Use caution in elderly patients with chronic kidney disease or those prone to electrolyte disturbances. Avoid unnecessary prolonged diuretic use when euvolemia is achieved to minimize risks.  
- **Monitoring:**  
  - Regularly check serum magnesium levels alongside sodium and potassium during diuretic therapy.  
  - Monitor renal function closely, as declining kidney function can worsen electrolyte imbalances.  
  - Assess clinical signs of magnesium deficiency, such as muscle weakness or arrhythmias.  
- **Management:**  
  - Adjust diuretic dose to the minimum effective amount to maintain euvolemia.  
  - Consider magnesium supplementation if deficiency is detected.  
  - Gradually deprescribe diuretics when appropriate, with close follow-up to detect recurrence of congestion or electrolyte disturbances.  
- **Additional considerations:** The elderly may have altered pharmacodynamics and increased sensitivity to electrolyte shifts; thus, individualized therapy and frequent reassessment are critical [1].
