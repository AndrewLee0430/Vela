## Summary

Elderly patients can generally take blood pressure (BP) medications with oral calcium supplements such as calcium citrate, as no clinically significant interactions or safety concerns were reported in a recent prospective study of elderly osteopenic subjects taking calcium citrate [2]. However, intravenous calcium chloride should be avoided with calcium channel blockers due to the risk of reduced antihypertensive response; if unavoidable, close blood pressure monitoring is required [1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Oral calcium citrate supplementation in elderly osteopenic subjects was well tolerated, with no serious adverse reactions and no reported interactions with BP medications in the cited study population [2].
- The most common adverse reactions to oral calcium citrate were mild gastrointestinal disorders (3.9%), and no cardiovascular safety signals were reported [2].
- For intravenous calcium chloride, avoid concomitant use with calcium channel blockers, as it may reduce the antihypertensive effect of these agents; if co-administration is unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration [1].
- Monitor for hypercalcemia when calcium is used with other drugs that increase calcium levels (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium) [1].
- There is no evidence in the provided context regarding interactions between oral calcium supplements and other classes of BP medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics except thiazides), so caution and routine monitoring are advised.
- Regularly monitor serum calcium levels in elderly patients on calcium supplementation, especially if they are also taking medications that affect calcium metabolism [1].
- The evidence for oral calcium citrate safety and tolerability is based on a 2024 study in elderly Caucasian osteopenic subjects; applicability to other populations or with other forms of calcium is not addressed [2].
