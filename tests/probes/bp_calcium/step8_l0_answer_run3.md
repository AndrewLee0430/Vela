## Summary  
Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications, including calcium channel blockers, but concomitant use with calcium chloride should be avoided due to potential interaction. If unavoidable, close monitoring of blood pressure is necessary during calcium chloride administration because calcium chloride may reduce the response to calcium channel blockers [1].

## Clinical Notes  
- Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers as it may reduce the antihypertensive effect of these drugs [1].  
- If co-administration is unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during calcium chloride administration to detect any diminished response or adverse effects [1].  
- Elderly patients often have altered pharmacodynamics and may be more sensitive to changes in calcium levels and blood pressure; therefore, careful clinical monitoring is essential.  
- There is no direct contraindication to elderly patients taking BP medications with calcium supplements in general, but specific calcium formulations like calcium chloride require caution when combined with calcium channel blockers [1].  
- Additional information on other BP medications and their interactions with calcium is not provided in the current context.
