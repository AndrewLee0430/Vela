## Summary

Elderly individuals are at increased risk of magnesium deficit, and the use of diuretics—especially those that cause increased urinary magnesium loss (hypermagnesuric diuretics)—is a significant contributor to secondary magnesium deficiency in this population[1][2]. Magnesium deficiency in the elderly can exacerbate neuromuscular, cardiovascular, and renal symptoms, and may play a role in the clinical manifestations of aging[1][2].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Safety Warnings and When NOT to Use:**  
  Care should be taken when prescribing diuretics to elderly patients, as these medications can increase urinary magnesium loss and precipitate or worsen magnesium deficiency[1][2]. This is particularly relevant for patients with pre-existing magnesium deficit or those at high risk (e.g., institutionalized elderly, those with poor dietary intake, or comorbidities such as diabetes mellitus)[1][2].

- **Key Contraindications:**  
  Use caution or avoid hypermagnesuric diuretics in elderly patients with known magnesium deficiency, significant renal impairment, or those with conditions that predispose to electrolyte disturbances[1][2]. Monitor for potential drug interactions that may further affect magnesium status.

- **Monitoring Parameters:**  
  - **Serum Magnesium:** Check baseline serum magnesium before starting diuretics and monitor periodically, especially in high-risk patients or those with symptoms suggestive of deficiency (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias)[1][2].
  - **Renal Function:** Assess renal function at baseline and regularly, as impaired renal function can alter magnesium handling and increase risk of both deficiency and toxicity[1][2].
  - **Clinical Symptoms:** Monitor for neuromuscular (e.g., muscle cramps, weakness), cardiovascular (e.g., arrhythmias), and renal symptoms that may indicate magnesium deficit[1][2].

- **Management:**  
  If magnesium deficiency is identified, oral physiological magnesium supplementation is recommended[1]. Addressing the underlying cause (e.g., adjusting diuretic therapy) is also important[1][2].

- **Evidence Limitations:**  
  The evidence is based on studies from 1993 and 1998; more recent data may be needed to confirm these findings in current elderly populations[1][2].
