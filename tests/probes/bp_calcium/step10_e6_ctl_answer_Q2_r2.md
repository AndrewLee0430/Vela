## Summary  
Taking potassium supplements or potassium-containing salt substitutes together with blood pressure (BP) medications such as angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) or drugs that inhibit the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) can increase the risk of hyperkalemia (high serum potassium). This is because these BP medications reduce aldosterone production, which normally promotes potassium excretion, leading to potassium retention. Therefore, potassium should be used cautiously and only under close medical supervision with frequent potassium monitoring if combined with these BP drugs [1][2][4].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Avoid concomitant use of potassium supplements with potassium-sparing diuretics** (e.g., amiloride, spironolactone) and RAAS inhibitors (ACE inhibitors, ARBs) due to high risk of severe hyperkalemia [1][3].  
- **Monitor serum potassium levels closely** if potassium supplementation is necessary while on BP medications that affect the RAAS system, especially ARBs like valsartan or losartan [1][2][4].  
- **NSAIDs can worsen potassium retention and renal function** when used with RAAS inhibitors and potassium supplements, increasing hyperkalemia risk and potentially reducing BP medication effectiveness. Renal function should be monitored periodically in such cases [1][2][4].  
- **Contraindications include patients with baseline hyperkalemia, severe renal impairment, or those on multiple potassium-increasing agents**.  
- **Monitoring parameters:**  
  - Check baseline serum potassium and renal function before starting or adding potassium supplements to BP medications.  
  - Repeat potassium and renal function tests frequently during therapy, especially after dose changes or adding interacting drugs.  
  - Discontinue or adjust potassium supplementation if serum potassium rises above the normal range (typically >5.0 mmol/L) or if renal function deteriorates.  
- **Lithium use with potassium-sparing diuretics or RAAS inhibitors requires caution** due to increased lithium toxicity risk [2][3][4].  

In summary, potassium supplementation with BP pills that affect the RAAS system is not absolutely contraindicated but requires careful risk-benefit assessment and close laboratory monitoring to prevent dangerous hyperkalemia.
