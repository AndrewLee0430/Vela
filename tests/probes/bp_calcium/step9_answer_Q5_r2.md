## Summary  
In elderly patients, diuretic use is commonly associated with electrolyte disturbances, including magnesium depletion, which can exacerbate clinical complications. Aging itself predisposes to magnesium deficiency due to reduced intake, impaired absorption, and increased renal losses, which are further aggravated by diuretics that promote magnesium wasting. This combined effect increases the risk of neuromuscular, cardiovascular, and renal symptoms in the elderly [1][2][3].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety warnings and when NOT to use:**  
  - Diuretics should be used cautiously in elderly patients due to the high risk of electrolyte imbalances, particularly hypomagnesemia, which can worsen morbidity and mortality [1].  
  - Avoid or closely monitor diuretic therapy in elderly patients with pre-existing magnesium deficiency or symptoms suggestive of magnesium depletion (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias).

- **Key contraindications:**  
  - Patients with known severe magnesium deficiency or those at risk of worsening magnesium depletion (e.g., elderly with poor nutrition, diabetes mellitus) require careful consideration before initiating diuretics [2][3].  
  - Use caution in patients with cardiac arrhythmias or neuromuscular disorders, as magnesium depletion can exacerbate these conditions.

- **Monitoring parameters:**  
  - Baseline serum magnesium should be checked before starting diuretics in elderly patients.  
  - Regular monitoring of serum magnesium, potassium, and sodium is recommended during diuretic therapy, especially in the elderly, to detect and correct electrolyte imbalances early [1].  
  - Monitor for clinical signs of magnesium deficiency such as muscle cramps, weakness, or cardiac arrhythmias.  
  - Consider magnesium supplementation if deficiency is detected or if the patient is at high risk due to diuretic use or aging-related factors [2][3].

- **Additional considerations:**  
  - Elderly patients often have multiple risk factors for magnesium depletion, including reduced dietary intake, impaired absorption, and comorbidities such as diabetes mellitus, which may be worsened by diuretic therapy [2][3].  
  - Addressing magnesium deficiency may improve neuromuscular and cardiovascular symptoms and potentially reduce morbidity in this population.  

The evidence is primarily from studies up to 2014 and earlier; more recent data may provide further insights but are not included here.
