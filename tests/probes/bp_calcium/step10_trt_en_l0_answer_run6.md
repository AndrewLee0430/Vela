## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections concomitantly with calcium channel blockers, as calcium chloride may reduce the response to these antihypertensive medications. If concomitant use is unavoidable, close monitoring of blood pressure is required during administration. There is insufficient information in the provided context regarding the safety of oral calcium supplements with other classes of blood pressure medications in the elderly [1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Chloride and Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use should be avoided because calcium chloride may reduce the antihypertensive effect of calcium channel blockers. If both must be used, monitor blood pressure closely during administration [1].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased risk of digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely [1].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** If calcium chloride is used with drugs such as calcipotriene, estrogen, lithium, parathyroid hormone, teriparatide, thiazide diuretics, Vitamin A, or Vitamin D, increase the frequency of calcium concentration monitoring to avoid hypercalcemia [1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** For patients receiving calcium chloride injection with interacting drugs, monitor blood pressure (if on calcium channel blockers), ECG (if on digoxin), and serum calcium levels (if on drugs increasing hypercalcemia risk) frequently during and after administration [1].
- **Missing Information:** The provided context does not address oral calcium supplements or interactions with other classes of blood pressure medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics other than thiazides), nor does it provide specific guidance for the elderly population outside of the above interactions [1], [2]. 

In summary, avoid combining calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers or digoxin in elderly patients unless absolutely necessary, and monitor closely if co-administration cannot be avoided. There is insufficient evidence in the provided context to comment on oral calcium with other blood pressure medications in the elderly.
