## Summary

Elderly patients can take vitamin D together with thiazide diuretics ("water pills"), but this combination increases the risk of hypercalcemia, especially in those with compromised kidney function or pre-existing hyperparathyroidism [1][2][5]. The risk is present with both oral and topical vitamin D preparations, and is higher in patients aged 70 years or older, those with chronic kidney disease, or those taking higher doses of vitamin D [2][5].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Safety Warnings and Contraindications:**
  - The combination of thiazide diuretics and vitamin D (oral or topical) increases serum calcium and the risk of hypercalcemia in elderly patients [1][2][5].
  - The risk is particularly elevated in patients with chronic kidney disease, hyperparathyroidism, or those over 70 years old [5].
  - Hypercalcemia can lead to symptoms such as confusion, muscle weakness, arrhythmias, and renal impairment.

- **Key Contraindications:**
  - Avoid or use with extreme caution in patients with known hypercalcemia, severe renal impairment, or primary hyperparathyroidism [1][2][5].
  - Thiazide diuretics should generally not be combined with lithium due to increased risk of lithium toxicity [3].

- **Monitoring Parameters:**
  - Check serum calcium at baseline and monitor periodically (e.g., after 1–3 months, then at regular intervals) during concurrent use [2][5].
  - Monitor renal function (eGFR) at baseline and during therapy, especially in elderly or those with known kidney disease [5].
  - If serum calcium rises above the upper limit of normal or if symptoms of hypercalcemia develop, discontinue one or both agents and reassess [2][5].
  - Consider more frequent monitoring in patients over 70, those with chronic kidney disease, or those on high-dose vitamin D [5].

- **Additional Notes:**
  - The evidence base is limited and mostly from observational studies or post hoc analyses, so findings should be interpreted with caution [1][2][5].
  - There is insufficient evidence to determine the exact risk threshold for vitamin D dosing in this population, but doses up to 4000 IU daily were associated with only a low frequency of hypercalcemia in one study of Black adults [2].
  - Drug interactions with other agents (e.g., NSAIDs, lithium, corticosteroids) should also be considered when prescribing thiazide diuretics [3].
