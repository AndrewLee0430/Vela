## Summary  
Elderly patients can take vitamin D supplements while on thiazide diuretics ("water pills"), but caution is needed due to an increased risk of hypercalcemia, especially in those with chronic kidney disease or advanced age (≥70 years). Thiazide diuretics reduce calcium excretion, and vitamin D increases calcium absorption, which together may elevate blood calcium levels. Therefore, monitoring calcium levels is important when these are used concurrently [5].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety warnings:**  
  - The combination of vitamin D (including topical vitamin D3 analogs) and thiazide diuretics can increase the risk of hypercalcemia, particularly in elderly patients (≥70 years) and those with chronic kidney disease [5].  
  - Hypercalcemia symptoms should be monitored, including confusion, weakness, nausea, and arrhythmias.  

- **Contraindications:**  
  - Avoid or use with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing hypercalcemia or severe renal impairment.  
  - Patients with chronic kidney disease are at higher risk and require closer monitoring [5].  

- **Drug interactions:**  
  - Thiazide diuretics reduce renal calcium clearance, potentiating hypercalcemia risk when combined with vitamin D supplements [1,5].  
  - Other drugs such as corticosteroids or lithium may also interact with thiazides and affect electrolyte balance [1].  

- **Monitoring parameters:**  
  - Check serum calcium levels before starting vitamin D supplementation in patients on thiazide diuretics.  
  - Regularly monitor serum calcium during combined therapy, especially in elderly patients and those with renal impairment.  
  - Monitor renal function (e.g., eGFR) as impaired kidney function increases hypercalcemia risk.  
  - Adjust vitamin D dose or thiazide therapy based on calcium levels and clinical status.  

- **Additional considerations:**  
  - Vitamin D status and dietary calcium intake do not consistently modify the effect of diuretics on bone health, but vitamin D supplementation remains important for bone health in the elderly [2,4].  
  - Patients should be advised to report symptoms suggestive of hypercalcemia promptly.  

In summary, vitamin D can be given with thiazide diuretics in elderly patients but requires careful clinical and laboratory monitoring to prevent and detect hypercalcemia early [5].
