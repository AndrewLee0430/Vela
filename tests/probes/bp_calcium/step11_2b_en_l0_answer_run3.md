## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and increased risk of hypercalcemia. Specifically, thiazide diuretics (such as hydrochlorothiazide) and calcium supplements or calcium chloride can increase the risk of hypercalcemia, and certain combinations (e.g., with digoxin or calcium channel blockers) require close monitoring or should be avoided if possible [1], [2].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Thiazide Diuretics and Calcium:** Thiazide diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide) reduce renal calcium excretion, which can increase serum calcium levels. When combined with calcium supplements or calcium chloride, there is a higher risk of hypercalcemia. Monitor serum calcium more frequently in patients on both agents [1], [2].
- **Calcium Chloride and Digoxin:** Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity, especially if hypercalcemia develops. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration [2].
- **Calcium Chloride and Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use may reduce the antihypertensive effect of calcium channel blockers. Avoid this combination if possible, or monitor blood pressure closely if used together [2].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** Additional drugs such as vitamin D, parathyroid hormone, and lithium can further increase the risk of hypercalcemia when used with calcium chloride or thiazide diuretics. Increase the frequency of calcium monitoring in these cases [2].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - Check serum calcium at baseline and periodically during therapy, especially when starting or adjusting doses of thiazide diuretics or calcium supplements.
  - Monitor for symptoms of hypercalcemia (e.g., confusion, weakness, arrhythmias).
  - For patients on digoxin or calcium channel blockers, monitor ECG and blood pressure as indicated [2].
- **Elderly Considerations:** Elderly patients are at higher risk for drug interactions and adverse effects due to age-related changes in renal function and drug metabolism. Dose adjustments and more frequent monitoring may be necessary [1], [2].

The provided context does not specify all BP medication classes or detail interactions with every possible antihypertensive agent. For combinations not discussed here, further evidence would be required.
