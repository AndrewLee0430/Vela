## Summary  
Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications, including calcium channel blockers, but concomitant use with calcium chloride should be avoided due to potential drug interactions. If unavoidable, close monitoring of blood pressure during calcium chloride administration is necessary because calcium chloride may reduce the response to calcium channel blockers [1].

## Clinical Notes  
- Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers as it may reduce the efficacy of the BP medication [1].  
- If co-administration is unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during calcium chloride administration to detect any diminished antihypertensive effect [1].  
- Elderly patients often have altered pharmacodynamics; thus, careful monitoring is especially important.  
- There is no direct contraindication to BP medications themselves in elderly patients, but the interaction with calcium chloride requires caution.  
- The provided context does not specify other classes of BP medications beyond calcium channel blockers, so no conclusions can be drawn about those.  
- Additional patient-specific factors and comorbidities should be considered when managing BP in elderly patients.
