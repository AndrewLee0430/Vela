## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections concomitantly with calcium channel blockers, as calcium chloride may reduce the response to these blood pressure (BP) medications. If concomitant use is unavoidable, close blood pressure monitoring is required during administration. There is no provided evidence regarding oral calcium supplements and their interaction with BP medications in elderly patients[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Chloride and Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers (such as amlodipine, diltiazem, or verapamil) may reduce the antihypertensive effect of the BP medication. Avoid this combination if possible; if unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration[1].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration[1].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** If calcium chloride injection is used with drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium), increase the frequency of calcium concentration monitoring[1].
- **Monitoring:** For patients on calcium chloride injection and BP medications, monitor blood pressure closely during administration. For those at risk of hypercalcemia, monitor serum calcium more frequently[1].
- **Oral Calcium Supplements:** The provided context does not address interactions between oral calcium supplements and BP medications in elderly patients. No recommendations can be made regarding this combination based on the current evidence[1], [2].
- **Population Scope:** The evidence specifically addresses calcium chloride injection, not oral calcium preparations, and does not provide data specific to elderly patients or to the broader population[1].

If considering calcium supplementation or calcium chloride injection in elderly patients on BP medications, careful assessment of drug interactions and monitoring is required.
