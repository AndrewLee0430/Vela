## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required, especially with calcium channel blockers and drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia. Concomitant use of calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers may reduce the effectiveness of the BP medication, and close blood pressure monitoring is recommended if co-administration is unavoidable[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection together with calcium channel blockers when possible, as calcium chloride may reduce the response to these antihypertensive agents. If co-administration cannot be avoided, monitor blood pressure closely during administration[1].
- **Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** If the patient is also taking other medications that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, parathyroid hormone, lithium), increase the frequency of calcium concentration monitoring to prevent complications[1].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid concomitant use of calcium chloride and digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration[1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - Monitor blood pressure closely if calcium chloride is used with calcium channel blockers.
  - Monitor serum calcium concentrations more frequently if the patient is on other drugs that increase hypercalcemia risk.
  - Monitor ECG if digoxin is also prescribed.
- **Contraindications and Safety:** There are no absolute contraindications to oral calcium with BP medications mentioned in the provided context, but injectable calcium chloride requires these precautions. The context does not specify for oral calcium or other forms.

The provided context does not address all BP medications or forms of calcium supplementation, nor does it specify recommendations unique to elderly patients beyond these drug interactions.
