## Summary

Concomitant use of potassium supplements or potassium-containing salt substitutes with spironolactone is contraindicated due to a high risk of severe hyperkalemia (elevated serum potassium), which can be life-threatening[1][4][5]. Spironolactone itself increases serum potassium by antagonizing aldosterone, and this effect is potentiated when combined with other potassium-elevating agents[1][4][5].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Contraindications and Safety Warnings:**  
  - Do not use potassium supplements or potassium-containing salt substitutes with spironolactone, as this combination can cause severe hyperkalemia[1][4].
  - Avoid combining spironolactone with other potassium-sparing diuretics or drugs that increase potassium (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, NSAIDs, co-trimoxazole, calcineurin inhibitors, β blockers) unless absolutely necessary and with close monitoring[1][4][5].
  - Spironolactone is also contraindicated in patients with significant renal impairment due to increased risk of hyperkalemia[1][5].

- **Monitoring Parameters:**  
  - Check serum potassium within one week of starting or titrating spironolactone, and monitor regularly thereafter[1].
  - More frequent potassium monitoring is required if spironolactone is used with other agents that increase potassium or in patients with impaired renal function[1][5].
  - Monitor renal function and volume status periodically, as worsening renal function can further increase hyperkalemia risk[1].
  - If hyperkalemia develops (serum potassium >5.0 mEq/L), reduce the dose or discontinue spironolactone and treat hyperkalemia as appropriate[1][2][5].

- **Drug Interactions:**  
  - Potassium-sparing diuretics and potassium supplements should be avoided with spironolactone[4].
  - Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, aliskiren) and NSAIDs increase potassium retention and require close potassium monitoring if used with spironolactone[4][5].
  - CYP3A4 inhibitors can increase the risk of hyperkalemia with most MRAs except spironolactone, but caution is still advised[5].

- **Management of Hyperkalemia:**  
  - Potassium-lowering therapies (e.g., loop or thiazide diuretics, novel potassium binders like sodium zirconium cyclosilicate) can be considered to enable continued use of spironolactone in patients at risk for or with mild hyperkalemia[3][5].
  - Individualized interventions and regular medication review are essential for safe MRA therapy[5].

- **Summary Table of Monitoring:**  
  - Serum potassium: Baseline, within 1 week of initiation/titration, then regularly (e.g., monthly for first 3 months, then every 3 months or as clinically indicated)[1][5].
  - Renal function (eGFR, creatinine): Baseline and periodically[1][5].
  - Volume status: Periodically[1].

Failure to adhere to these precautions can result in life-threatening hyperkalemia and cardiac arrhythmias[1][4][5].
