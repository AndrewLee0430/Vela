## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections concomitantly with calcium channel blockers, as this may reduce the effectiveness of the blood pressure medication. If co-administration is unavoidable, close blood pressure monitoring is required during administration. There is no direct evidence provided regarding oral calcium supplements with other classes of blood pressure medications in the elderly[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Chloride and Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use is not recommended because calcium chloride may reduce the response to calcium channel blockers. If unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration[1].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased risk of digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If co-administration is unavoidable, monitor ECG closely[1].
- **Other BP Medications:** The provided context does not address interactions between calcium (oral or intravenous) and other antihypertensive classes (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics) in the elderly.
- **Hypercalcemia Risk:** If calcium chloride is given with drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium), increase the frequency of calcium concentration monitoring[1].
- **Monitoring:** For patients on calcium chloride injection and interacting medications, monitor blood pressure and ECG as indicated, and check serum calcium levels more frequently if there is a risk of hypercalcemia[1].

**Missing Information:** The context does not provide data on the safety or interactions of oral calcium supplements with blood pressure medications in elderly patients, nor does it address non-calcium channel blocker antihypertensives.
