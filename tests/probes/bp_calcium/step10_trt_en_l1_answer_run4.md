## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections concomitantly with calcium channel blockers, as calcium chloride may reduce the response to these antihypertensive medications and requires close blood pressure monitoring if co-administration is unavoidable [1]. There is no specific information provided about oral calcium supplements and other classes of blood pressure medications in the supplied context.

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers (such as amlodipine, diltiazem, or verapamil) should be avoided because calcium chloride may reduce the antihypertensive effect of these drugs [1]. If co-administration cannot be avoided, blood pressure should be monitored closely during administration [1].
- **Digoxin:** Calcium chloride injection should not be used with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased risk of digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, ECG monitoring is required during administration [1].
- **Other Blood Pressure Medications:** The provided context does not address interactions between calcium (oral or injectable) and other antihypertensive classes (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, thiazide diuretics, etc.).
- **Hypercalcemia Risk:** If calcium chloride is used with other drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium), increase the frequency of calcium concentration monitoring [1].
- **Monitoring:** For patients on calcium chloride injection and interacting medications, monitor blood pressure and/or ECG as indicated, and check serum calcium levels more frequently if there is a risk of hypercalcemia [1].
