## Summary  
In elderly patients taking blood pressure (BP) medications, daily low-dose aspirin (100 mg) does not significantly affect blood pressure levels or interfere with antihypertensive treatment efficacy. A large randomized trial in community-dwelling older adults showed no meaningful difference in systolic or diastolic BP changes between aspirin and placebo groups over nearly 5 years, regardless of baseline BP or antihypertensive use [1]. Earlier evidence also indicates aspirin is among the safest nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) regarding antagonism of antihypertensive drugs, unlike other NSAIDs such as indomethacin which can raise BP by interfering with antihypertensive therapy [2].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety and Use:** Low-dose aspirin (100 mg daily) can be safely used in elderly patients on antihypertensive medications without concern for raising blood pressure or reducing antihypertensive drug effectiveness [1,2].  
- **Contraindications:** Aspirin should still be used cautiously in patients with bleeding risks or other contraindications unrelated to BP effects. The provided data do not address aspirin use in patients with severe renal impairment or active bleeding.  
- **Drug Interactions:** Unlike some NSAIDs (e.g., indomethacin), aspirin does not antagonize the blood pressure-lowering effects of beta-blockers, diuretics, ACE inhibitors, or other antihypertensive drugs [2].  
- **Monitoring:** Routine BP monitoring remains essential in elderly hypertensive patients, but no additional BP-specific monitoring is required solely due to aspirin use. No significant changes in BP or incident hypertension were observed with aspirin over 4.7 years [1].  
- **Clinical Context:** These findings apply to community-dwelling older adults without cardiovascular disease or disabling conditions, as studied in the ASPREE trial [1]. Effects in other populations or those with established CVD may differ and were not addressed here.
