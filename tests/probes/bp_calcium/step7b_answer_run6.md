## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions and increased risk of hypercalcemia. Calcium chloride can interact with several BP medications, particularly thiazide diuretics and calcium channel blockers, necessitating close monitoring of calcium levels and cardiovascular status [2], [3].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Thiazide Diuretics (e.g., Hydrochlorothiazide):** Thiazide diuretics can increase serum calcium levels by reducing renal calcium excretion. When combined with calcium supplements or calcium chloride, there is a higher risk of hypercalcemia. Monitor serum calcium concentrations more frequently in patients taking both thiazide diuretics and calcium [2].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Concomitant use of calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers may reduce the antihypertensive effect of the calcium channel blocker. If unavoidable, monitor blood pressure closely during administration [2], [3].
- **Digoxin:** Avoid using calcium chloride with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity, especially if hypercalcemia develops. If co-administration is unavoidable, monitor ECG closely [2].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** Additional agents such as vitamin D, parathyroid hormone, and lithium can further increase the risk of hypercalcemia when used with calcium chloride. Increase the frequency of calcium monitoring in these scenarios [2].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - Check serum calcium at baseline and periodically during therapy, especially after starting or adjusting calcium or thiazide diuretic doses.
  - Monitor for symptoms of hypercalcemia (e.g., confusion, muscle weakness, arrhythmias).
  - For patients on digoxin or calcium channel blockers, monitor ECG and blood pressure during co-administration [2].
- **Elderly Considerations:** Elderly patients may have reduced renal function, increasing the risk of hypercalcemia and drug accumulation. Dose adjustments and more frequent monitoring may be necessary.
- **Contraindications:** Avoid calcium chloride in patients with hypercalcemia or ventricular fibrillation [2].

The provided context does not specify detailed protocols for all BP medication classes or address all possible comorbidities in elderly patients. Clinical judgment and individualized monitoring are essential.
