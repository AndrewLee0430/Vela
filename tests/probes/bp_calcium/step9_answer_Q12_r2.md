## Summary  
Spironolactone use carries a risk of hyperkalemia, especially when combined with potassium-sparing diuretics, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors (such as ACE inhibitors or ARBs), or NSAIDs, which all promote potassium retention. Monitoring potassium levels is important in patients with risk factors such as impaired renal function, diabetes, or proteinuria. However, in healthy young women treated with spironolactone for acne, potassium monitoring is often of low yield and has decreased over time in clinical practice. In transgender women on spironolactone, significant hyperkalemia (>5.5 mmol/L) is rare when renal function is normal [1][2][3][4].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Contraindications and Cautions:**  
  - Avoid concomitant use of spironolactone with other potassium-sparing diuretics due to risk of severe hyperkalemia [1].  
  - Use caution and closely monitor potassium when spironolactone is combined with RAAS inhibitors (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, eplerenone, aliskiren) or NSAIDs, as these increase potassium retention [1][2].  
  - Patients with decreased renal function, diabetes, or proteinuria are at higher risk of hyperkalemia and require careful monitoring [2].  
- **Monitoring Recommendations:**  
  - Baseline serum potassium should be checked before starting spironolactone.  
  - Potassium levels should be monitored periodically, especially in patients with risk factors or on interacting drugs [1][2].  
  - In healthy young women treated for acne, routine potassium monitoring may be of low clinical utility, and many clinicians have reduced monitoring frequency accordingly [3].  
  - In transgender women with normal renal function, significant hyperkalemia is uncommon; routine potassium monitoring remains prudent but may be less frequently needed if no risk factors are present [4].  
- **Dose Adjustments:**  
  - If hyperkalemia develops (potassium 5.5-5.9 mEq/L), dose reduction of spironolactone may be considered with continued monitoring [2].  
  - When moderate CYP3A inhibitors are used concomitantly, spironolactone dose reduction is recommended to minimize hyperkalemia risk [2].  
- **Safety Warnings:**  
  - Avoid spironolactone in patients with severe renal impairment or baseline hyperkalemia.  
  - Monitor for signs of hyperkalemia (muscle weakness, arrhythmias) and intervene promptly.  

In summary, potassium monitoring with spironolactone should be individualized based on patient risk factors and concomitant medications, with more intensive monitoring in high-risk populations and less frequent monitoring in healthy young women without risk factors.
