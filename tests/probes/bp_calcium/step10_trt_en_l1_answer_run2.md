## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections together with blood pressure medications that are calcium channel blockers, as this combination may reduce the effectiveness of the blood pressure medication and requires close monitoring if unavoidable. If co-administration cannot be avoided, blood pressure should be closely monitored during calcium chloride administration[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection with calcium channel blockers (a common class of blood pressure medications) is not recommended because calcium chloride may reduce the response to these medications[1].
- If it is unavoidable to use both, blood pressure should be monitored closely during administration of calcium chloride injection[1].
- Calcium chloride injection should also be avoided with digoxin due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased risk of digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia; if used together, ECG monitoring is required[1].
- For patients taking other drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium), increase the frequency of calcium concentration monitoring[1].
- The provided context does not address oral calcium supplements or other classes of blood pressure medications (such as ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or beta-blockers), so recommendations are limited to calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers.
