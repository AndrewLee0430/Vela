## Summary  
Calcium channel blockers and lisinopril can be coadministered without significant pharmacokinetic interactions, and their antihypertensive effects are generally additive. However, hydralazine, another vasodilator, may affect lisinopril pharmacokinetics, but this does not directly apply to calcium channel blockers [2].

## Clinical Notes  
- No significant pharmacokinetic interaction exists between calcium antagonists (calcium channel blockers) and ACE inhibitors like lisinopril, so dose adjustments solely due to interaction are typically unnecessary [2].  
- The combination may provide additive blood pressure lowering effects, which can be beneficial in hypertension management. Monitor blood pressure to avoid hypotension.  
- There is a report of hydralazine affecting lisinopril pharmacokinetics, but this does not extend to calcium channel blockers [2].  
- Monitor renal function and electrolytes as usual when using lisinopril, especially in patients with renal impairment or those on other antihypertensives.  
- The provided data do not specify any safety concerns or contraindications unique to the combination of calcium channel blockers and lisinopril.  
- Additional monitoring or dose adjustment may be needed based on clinical response rather than pharmacokinetic interaction.  

The evidence is from a 1993 pharmacokinetic review and may not include newer agents or formulations [2].
