## Summary

In community-dwelling older adults without cardiovascular disease, adding daily low-dose aspirin (100 mg) to blood pressure (BP) medication does not significantly change systolic or diastolic BP compared to placebo over a median of 4.7 years (mean difference in systolic BP change: -0.03 mm Hg, 95% CI: -0.13 to 0.07; diastolic BP: -0.05 mm Hg, 95% CI: -0.11 to 0.01) [1]. Aspirin also does not alter the incidence of new-onset treated hypertension in this population [1]. The mechanism appears to be a lack of clinically meaningful interaction between aspirin and antihypertensive therapy on BP control in older adults without established cardiovascular disease.

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Safety Warnings and Contraindications:** Aspirin should not be used in older adults with a history of significant gastrointestinal bleeding, active peptic ulcer disease, bleeding disorders, or known hypersensitivity to aspirin [3]. The risk-benefit profile should be carefully considered, especially in primary prevention, due to the increased risk of bleeding in the elderly [3].
- **Drug Interactions:** Aspirin may increase the risk of bleeding when combined with anticoagulants or other antiplatelet agents. Caution is also warranted with concomitant use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) or corticosteroids [3].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - Monitor for signs of gastrointestinal bleeding (e.g., melena, hematemesis) and unexplained anemia periodically.
  - Renal function should be assessed at baseline and periodically, especially in those with pre-existing chronic kidney disease or concomitant nephrotoxic medications.
  - Blood pressure should continue to be monitored as per standard hypertension management guidelines; aspirin does not necessitate additional BP monitoring beyond usual care [1].
- **Population Applicability:** The evidence is based on community-dwelling older adults (mean age ~75) without cardiovascular disease, dementia, or significant physical disability, primarily from Australia and the US [1]. Results may not generalize to frailer populations, those with established CVD, or non-Western settings.
- **No BP Effect:** Aspirin does not meaningfully alter BP or the development of hypertension when added to antihypertensive therapy in this population [1]. There is no need to adjust antihypertensive regimens solely due to aspirin use.
- **Bleeding Risk:** The decision to use aspirin for primary prevention in the elderly should weigh the modest cardiovascular benefit against the increased risk of bleeding, particularly gastrointestinal [3]. Regular review of indication is recommended.
