## Summary  
Lisinopril, an ACE inhibitor, is effective in lowering blood pressure and providing renal protection in diabetic patients, with additional benefits in slowing retinopathy progression and possibly improving neurological function. Calcium channel blockers, while also antihypertensive, appear to have less renoprotective effect compared to lisinopril in diabetic nephropathy. Both drug classes are commonly used in hypertension management but differ in their impact on diabetic complications [2]. There is a concern about nitrosamine contamination in calcium channel blockers (e.g., amlodipine, felodipine) and other antihypertensives including lisinopril, which may be linked to increased cancer risks, although this is a broader safety issue affecting multiple drug classes [1].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Safety and Use:**  
  - Lisinopril is well tolerated in diabetic and non-diabetic patients, with a safety profile typical of ACE inhibitors. It does not adversely affect glycemic control or lipid profiles [2].  
  - Calcium channel blockers are effective antihypertensives but may have less benefit in renal protection compared to lisinopril in diabetic nephropathy [2].  
  - Both drug classes are widely used in hypertension, but clinicians should be aware of recent concerns about nitrosamine contamination in these medications, which has been implicated in increased risks of various cancers including skin cancer [1]. This contamination issue is a global problem affecting many commonly prescribed drugs.  

- **Contraindications and Monitoring:**  
  - Lisinopril should be avoided in patients with a history of angioedema related to ACE inhibitors and in pregnancy. Monitor renal function and potassium levels regularly, as ACE inhibitors can cause hyperkalemia and renal impairment.  
  - Calcium channel blockers should be used cautiously in patients with severe hypotension or certain cardiac conduction abnormalities.  
  - Given the nitrosamine contamination risk, ongoing pharmacovigilance and regulatory updates should be monitored to guide safe prescribing.  

- **Monitoring Parameters:**  
  - For lisinopril: baseline and periodic monitoring of serum creatinine and potassium is recommended. Dose adjustments may be needed if renal function declines.  
  - For calcium channel blockers: monitor blood pressure and heart rate; watch for peripheral edema and other adverse effects.  
  - Awareness of potential long-term carcinogenic risks due to nitrosamine contamination should prompt consideration of alternative agents if contamination is confirmed in specific drug batches [1].  

The provided data do not include direct comparative safety data regarding nitrosamine contamination levels between lisinopril and calcium channel blockers, nor specific clinical guidance on managing this contamination risk beyond awareness [1],[2].
