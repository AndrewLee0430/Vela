## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections together with blood pressure medications that are calcium channel blockers, as this combination may reduce the effectiveness of the blood pressure medication. If concomitant use is unavoidable, close monitoring of blood pressure is required during administration of calcium chloride injection[1]. The mechanism involves calcium chloride potentially antagonizing the effects of calcium channel blockers.

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Safety Warnings:** Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers should be avoided due to the risk of reduced antihypertensive response[1].
- **Key Contraindications:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection with digoxin, as this combination can increase the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and digoxin toxicity, especially in the presence of hypercalcemia[1].
- **Drug Interactions:** Calcium chloride injection may interact with other drugs that increase the risk of hypercalcemia (e.g., calcipotriene, estrogen, lithium, parathyroid hormone, teriparatide, thiazide diuretics, Vitamin A, and Vitamin D). Increased monitoring of calcium concentrations is recommended if these drugs are used together[1].
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - If calcium chloride injection must be used with a calcium channel blocker, monitor blood pressure closely during administration[1].
  - If used with digoxin, monitor ECG closely during administration[1].
  - Increase frequency of serum calcium monitoring if used with other agents that raise calcium levels[1].
- **Missing Context:** The provided evidence specifically addresses calcium chloride injection, not oral calcium supplements, and does not specify which blood pressure medications beyond calcium channel blockers are affected. There is also no elderly-specific data provided in the context[1].
