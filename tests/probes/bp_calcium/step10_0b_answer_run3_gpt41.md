## Summary

Elderly patients should generally avoid taking calcium chloride injections together with blood pressure medications, especially calcium channel blockers, due to potential drug interactions. If concomitant use is unavoidable, close monitoring of blood pressure is required, as calcium chloride may reduce the response to calcium channel blockers[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Concomitant use of calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers should be avoided because calcium chloride may reduce the antihypertensive effect of these medications[1].
- If it is necessary to use both, blood pressure should be monitored closely during administration of calcium chloride injection[1].
- Calcium chloride injection can also increase the risk of hypercalcemia, especially when used with other drugs that raise calcium levels (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium, parathyroid hormone, calcipotriene, estrogen, teriparatide, vitamin A)[1]. In such cases, increase the frequency of monitoring serum calcium concentrations.
- For patients on digoxin, avoid concomitant use with calcium chloride injection due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If unavoidable, monitor ECG closely during administration[1].
- The provided context does not address oral calcium supplements or other classes of blood pressure medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers), so caution is advised when extrapolating these recommendations beyond calcium chloride injection and calcium channel blockers.
