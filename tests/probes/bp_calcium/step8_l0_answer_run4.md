## Summary  
Elderly patients can take blood pressure medications including calcium channel blockers, but clinicians must be cautious about potential drug-drug interactions and additive effects with other antihypertensives. Close monitoring is essential to avoid adverse effects such as hypotension or altered drug efficacy, especially when combined with other agents like diuretics or NSAIDs [1][2].

## Clinical Notes  
- Calcium channel blockers are commonly used in elderly patients for hypertension and other cardiovascular conditions, often in combination therapy [2].  
- Combination therapy increases the risk of pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions; therefore, careful assessment and monitoring are required [2].  
- When calcium channel blockers are used with other antihypertensive drugs such as thiazide diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide), additive blood pressure-lowering effects may occur, increasing the risk of hypotension [1].  
- Diuretics like hydrochlorothiazide can interact with other drugs (e.g., NSAIDs may reduce diuretic efficacy; corticosteroids may worsen electrolyte depletion) and require monitoring of electrolytes and renal function [1].  
- Elderly patients are more vulnerable to orthostatic hypotension, especially when combining antihypertensives with alcohol, barbiturates, or narcotics [1].  
- Monitoring parameters should include blood pressure, renal function, and electrolytes regularly, with dose adjustments as needed.  
- There is no explicit contraindication to calcium channel blockers in elderly patients, but vigilance for interactions with food, supplements, and other medications is necessary [2].  
- Specific guidance on dose adjustments or monitoring frequency is not provided in the available context and should be individualized based on patient response and comorbidities.
