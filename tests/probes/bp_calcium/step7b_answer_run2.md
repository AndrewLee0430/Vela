## Summary

Elderly patients can generally take blood pressure medications (BP meds) with calcium, but caution is needed due to the potential for drug-drug and drug-food interactions, especially with calcium channel blockers (CCBs) [1]. The risk of interactions increases with combination therapy, which is common in this population.

---

## Clinical Notes

- Calcium channel blockers are widely used in elderly patients for hypertension and other cardiovascular conditions, but the risk of clinically significant drug-drug and drug-food interactions is higher when combined with other medications or supplements, including calcium [1].
- Clinicians should be vigilant for pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions when prescribing BP meds alongside calcium supplements or calcium-rich foods [1].
- The provided source does not specify exact contraindications or detailed monitoring parameters for the combination of BP meds and calcium in the elderly.
- It is important to monitor for signs of altered drug efficacy or adverse effects when calcium is added to a regimen containing BP meds, particularly CCBs [1].
