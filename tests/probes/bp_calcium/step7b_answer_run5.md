## Summary

Elderly patients can take blood pressure (BP) medications with calcium, but caution is required due to potential drug interactions. Specifically, concomitant use of calcium chloride with calcium channel blockers should generally be avoided, as calcium may reduce the antihypertensive effect of these medications and increase the risk of adverse effects such as hypercalcemia or arrhythmias, especially in patients also taking digoxin [1], [2].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Avoid using calcium chloride injection together with calcium channel blockers if possible, as calcium can reduce the response to these antihypertensive agents. If concomitant use is unavoidable, blood pressure should be monitored closely during administration [1].
- **Digoxin:** Concomitant use of calcium chloride and digoxin should be avoided due to the risk of synergistic arrhythmias and increased digoxin toxicity from hypercalcemia. If used together, close ECG monitoring is required [1].
- **Other Drugs Increasing Hypercalcemia Risk:** If calcium is given with other drugs that increase serum calcium (e.g., thiazide diuretics, vitamin D, lithium), increase the frequency of serum calcium monitoring to detect hypercalcemia early [1].
- **Elderly Considerations:** Elderly patients are at higher risk for drug-drug interactions and may have reduced renal function, increasing the risk of hypercalcemia and toxicity. Monitor serum calcium and renal function regularly.
- **Monitoring Parameters:** 
  - Check serum calcium at baseline and periodically during therapy, especially if combined with drugs affecting calcium metabolism.
  - Monitor blood pressure and ECG if using with calcium channel blockers or digoxin.
- **Contraindications:** Do not use calcium chloride in patients with hypercalcemia or ventricular fibrillation [1].
- **Insufficient Context:** The provided sources do not address all BP medications (e.g., ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, ARBs) or oral calcium supplements. The evidence specifically addresses calcium chloride injection and its interactions.

If considering calcium supplementation or intravenous calcium in elderly patients on BP medications, individual risk assessment and close monitoring are essential.
