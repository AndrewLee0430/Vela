## Summary

Diuretic use in elderly patients can lead to alterations in magnesium levels, contributing to electrolyte and acid-base disorders[1]. Careful monitoring is essential, as elderly individuals are at increased risk for these diuretic-associated abnormalities, which can result in significant morbidity and mortality[1].

---

## Clinical Notes

- Diuretics may cause disturbances in magnesium homeostasis in elderly patients, alongside other electrolyte imbalances such as hyponatraemia, hypokalaemia, and hypernatraemia[1].
- The elderly are particularly susceptible to these effects due to age-related physiological changes and comorbidities[1].
- Regular monitoring of serum magnesium, along with sodium, potassium, and other relevant electrolytes, is recommended during diuretic therapy in older adults[1].
- Monitoring should be performed at baseline and periodically during treatment; the frequency should increase if the patient has additional risk factors or develops symptoms suggestive of electrolyte disturbance[1].
- Prevention strategies include careful risk assessment before starting diuretics and ongoing clinical and laboratory evaluation throughout therapy[1].
- The article does not provide specific magnesium threshold values or detailed monitoring intervals, nor does it specify which diuretics most commonly affect magnesium in the elderly[1].
- Evidence is based on literature up to 2014; more recent data may not be included[1].
