## Summary

Grapefruit juice significantly increases the bioavailability of several blood pressure (BP) medications, especially dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers such as felodipine, by inhibiting intestinal CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, leading to higher plasma drug concentrations and potentially exaggerated pharmacologic effects [1], [2], [3], [5]. This interaction can result in increased risk of adverse effects, including hypotension and related complications [1], [2], [5].

---

## Clinical Notes

- **Key BP Medications Affected:** Most dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (e.g., felodipine, nifedipine, amlodipine), and verapamil, are susceptible to clinically relevant interactions with grapefruit juice [1], [2], [5]. Other antihypertensives are not specifically mentioned in the provided context.
- **Mechanism:** Grapefruit juice inhibits intestinal CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, reducing presystemic metabolism and increasing absorption of affected drugs [1], [2], [5]. This leads to higher AUC and Cmax for drugs like felodipine (AUC increased from 28.1 to 56.6 ng·ml⁻¹·h, Cmax from 3.3 to 8.1 ng/ml with grapefruit juice vs. water) [3].
- **Safety Warnings:** Avoid coadministration of grapefruit juice with susceptible BP medications due to risk of excessive hypotension, dizziness, and other dose-related adverse effects [1], [2], [5]. Even a single glass can produce maximal interaction [5].
- **Contraindications:** Do not use grapefruit juice with dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers or verapamil. Use caution with other drugs metabolized by CYP3A4, especially if they have a narrow therapeutic index [1], [2], [5].
- **Monitoring:** If inadvertent coadministration occurs, monitor for signs of hypotension (e.g., dizziness, syncope), and consider checking drug plasma levels if available. Advise patients to avoid grapefruit products entirely while on affected medications [2], [5].
- **Duration of Effect:** The interaction can last up to 24 hours after grapefruit juice consumption, and repeated intake can have cumulative effects [1].
- **Patient Variability:** The magnitude of interaction varies between individuals, depending on baseline intestinal CYP3A4 expression and the amount/type of grapefruit product consumed [1], [2].
- **Other Notes:** The evidence base is primarily from studies in healthy adults; effects in other populations (elderly, comorbidities) are not detailed in the provided context.

If considering a BP medication for a patient who regularly consumes grapefruit juice, select agents not metabolized by CYP3A4 or advise complete avoidance of grapefruit products.
