## Clinical Notes

- The provided context does not list calcium or calcium supplements as interacting with ACE inhibitors like lisinopril[1].
- Key drug interactions for ACE inhibitors include:
  - Increased risk of angioedema with neprilysin inhibitors.
  - Increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and renal dysfunction when combined with other renin-angiotensin system (RAS) inhibitors (e.g., ARBs, aliskiren). Dual RAS blockade is generally not recommended.
  - Do not coadminister aliskiren with ACE inhibitors in patients with diabetes, and avoid in those with renal impairment (GFR <60 mL/min).
  - Patients on diuretics may experience excessive hypotension when starting ACE inhibitors; consider adjusting diuretic therapy or monitoring closely after the first dose.
  - NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors) may worsen renal function when used with ACE inhibitors, especially in elderly, volume-depleted, or renally impaired patients.
- Monitoring parameters for patients on ACE inhibitors:
  - Blood pressure: monitor closely, especially after initiation or dose changes.
  - Renal function (e.g., serum creatinine, eGFR): check at baseline and periodically thereafter.
  - Serum potassium: monitor for hyperkalemia, particularly in patients with renal impairment or those on potassium-sparing agents.
- No monitoring or dose adjustment is indicated for calcium supplementation based on the provided context[1].
