## Summary  
Taking potassium supplements or potassium-containing salt substitutes together with blood pressure (BP) medications such as angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) or ACE inhibitors can increase the risk of hyperkalemia (high potassium levels in the blood). This is because these BP medications reduce aldosterone production, which normally promotes potassium excretion, leading to potassium retention. Therefore, potassium should be used cautiously and only under close medical supervision with frequent monitoring of serum potassium levels when combined with these drugs [1][2][3][4].

## Clinical Notes  
- **Avoid concomitant use of potassium supplements with potassium-sparing diuretics** (e.g., amiloride, spironolactone) and RAAS inhibitors (ACE inhibitors, ARBs) due to risk of severe hyperkalemia [1][4].  
- **Monitor serum potassium closely** if potassium supplements are necessary alongside BP medications that inhibit the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), such as valsartan or losartan [1][2][3].  
- **NSAIDs can also increase potassium levels** and impair renal function when used with these BP drugs, so potassium monitoring and renal function assessment are advised if NSAIDs are co-administered [1][2][3][4].  
- **Check renal function periodically** (e.g., serum creatinine, eGFR) because combined use of potassium supplements and RAAS inhibitors can worsen kidney function, especially in elderly or volume-depleted patients [2][3].  
- **Avoid dual blockade of RAAS** (e.g., combining ACE inhibitors with ARBs) as it increases risks of hyperkalemia, hypotension, and renal impairment [2][3].  
- **If you are on lithium therapy, caution is needed** because potassium-sparing diuretics and RAAS inhibitors can increase lithium toxicity risk [3][4].  
- **Summary of monitoring:** Baseline and frequent serum potassium and renal function tests are essential when potassium supplements are taken with BP medications that affect potassium homeostasis. Adjust or discontinue potassium supplementation if hyperkalemia develops.  

In conclusion, do not take potassium supplements with your BP pills without medical advice and laboratory monitoring due to the significant risk of hyperkalemia and renal complications.
